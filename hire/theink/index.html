0ff The Print / Interview 02

TheInk

Smiley Onerr · @smiley_one98
Legion Ink · 719 N New Braunfels Ave · San Antonio

What it is

You tattooing, start to finish, talking while you work. Four to six minutes. The machine is running through the whole thing.

Nothing gets staged for me, nothing gets moved, and you don't have to perform. You work, I ask you stuff, the camera stays out of your light.

We're doing it across two days. Friday I just shoot the shop and you working, no interview, nothing you have to get ready for. The real one is its own day, with the client you're lining up, and that's the day we actually sit down and talk.

  • Not a commercial for the shop
  • Not a talking head against a blank wall
  • Not scripted, rehearsed, or done in takes

How it runs

Two days, and the second one is the one

Day one · FridayAbout two hours. No interview. Nothing to prepare.
  1. Before you start

    The shop empty

    I get there early and shoot the room with nobody in it. Flash on the walls, the tray, the chair, the front door. This is the opening of the video.

  2. Two minutes

    Paper

    You sign one page, and whoever owns the shop signs one. Plain English, one page each, nothing about owning your work.

  3. Whatever you're already booked for

    You working

    Stencil, machine, your hands, the wipe. I'll put a mic on you and leave it running, but I'm not interviewing you today. If you feel like talking, talk. If you don't, don't.

Day two · the interviewAbout three hours, with the client you're setting up.
  1. Client in the chair

    The session

    Start to finish, same as any other day you work. Your client seeing it in the mirror at the end is the ending of the piece.

  2. 30 to 40 minutes

    Sitting down

    This is the part I want. You in your own chair at your own station, not against a blank wall, and I get to actually ask you things instead of catching you between lines. Your answers are what the whole piece is built on.

  3. Last 5 minutes

    Just you

    Shop quiet, client gone. Stills of you at the station. Those are the photos that go on the site and on your card.

Where the cameras sit

Two, and neither one is in your way

No lights in your face. Shop light is the look. Your work is black and grey and I am not putting some color filter over the top of it and fighting the piece.

What it sounds like

The machine is the soundtrack

No music laid over your voice, no narrator, no questions read off a card. It's a conversation, not a quiz, and you'll have already been on camera a whole day by the time we get to it. Three of the kind of things I'll ask, so you know what you're walking into:

  • Where does Smiley come from? Who gave it to you?
  • Why black and grey and not color?
  • What's the piece you'd redo if you could?
House rule Anything you don't want in it, say so and it's gone. Doesn't matter if it's already on camera. Off the record stays off the record, that's a real rule here, not a line.

The trade

What you get, what I need

You get

  • The finished piece, 4 to 6 minutes
  • Three vertical cuts for your page
  • Stills of you, the station, and the tattoo
  • All of it before it goes anywhere public
  • No charge, ever, for any of it

I need

  • Friday: a couple hours while you work
  • Day two: the client you're lining up, cool with being filmed
  • Forearm, hand or neck piece if you get any say in it
  • The shop owner's OK on me shooting there
  • 30 to 40 minutes of you sitting down on day two

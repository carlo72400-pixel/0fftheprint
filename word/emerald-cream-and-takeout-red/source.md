The flyer was emerald paper with cream lettering, warm black ink, a takeout red and a little gold. The shop looked exactly like it. Exotic Takeout on Perrin Beitel Road dresses up as a Chinese takeout. There's a lucky cat on the sign, red takeout boxes on the shelf and the name painted big across the front wall. CHILL & SMOKE ran from 5 to 9 on a Sunday with a DJ on the decks, and the camera got there early, at a quarter to four, while the room was still a shop.

So the first frames are the details. The lucky cat on the sign. A red takeout box that says Enjoy. A pair of OutKast action figures still in the package. A rug by the counter that says SORRY NO FRONTS. Then the couches filled up and the decks started. By dusk the whole crew was lined up out front under the mural for the group photo.

## A room that's already graded

Most nights the grade has to invent a look. This one came printed on the flyer. 02 CHILL & SMOKE is built off those five colors. We keyed the plants in the room to the flyer's emerald, and we gave the whites a ceiling so they print like cream paper instead of blowing out. The blacks sit on a warm ink floor.

That last part was a fix. Our first version tinted the shadows green to match the paper. Every black merch tee in the room went teal. So black stays the flyer's ink. Reds and gold get richer, and blues lean teal but stay off the dark pixels. Skin gets its color handed back at the very end, never more than 4 percent. In Photoshop we laid on a cream halation at 13 percent. That's the smoke the night was named for, and it goes through a mask that keeps it off faces.

01 NATURAL is the same frames as they were. Lens, white balance, exposure, color, tone.

## What the raw files asked for

We developed every frame from its RAW file. On this camera that comes with a cost. The RAW render carries about four times the color noise in the shadows that the camera's own JPEG does. That's true at every ISO on the card, from 128 up to 1025, not only the high ones. So every frame got a color denoise, not just the dark ones.

We read white balance off the walls, since they're neutral in this room, and it moved on 77 of 79 frames, by 2.9 percent on the median. Exposure didn't need a single global move once each frame was anchored on its own. The camera had tagged seven frames sideways, so we turned them upright. We cropped 12 zooms that landed between the lens steps back to the framing we meant. Grain on both looks is color negative film, a touch lighter on the natural set.

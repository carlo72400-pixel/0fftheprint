In June, Google put about seventy five million dollars into A24. Google DeepMind called it a first of its kind research partnership between a world leading research lab and the industry's most filmmaker forward studio. The first thing they named as a use case was storyboards.

The horror internet took roughly one afternoon to decide this was the end of something.

## What the deal actually is

Worth being accurate, because the panic ran ahead of the paperwork. Google is not buying A24. The studio stays independent and the deal is not exclusive. The Hollywood Reporter's read was that the agreement does not hand Google access to A24's library or its data, which is the part everybody assumed had been sold.

Scott Belsky, who runs tech at A24, told IndieWire that whatever comes out of it will not look anything like the prompted generation type of AI that people feel uncomfortable with.

That is a good sentence. It is also a sentence a studio says before anybody has seen the output.

## Why people are not letting it go

Because it is A24. This is the house that put out The Witch and Hereditary and Midsommar, built on the argument that you back a specific person with a specific eye and get out of the way. Seventy five million dollars from a search company is not a neutral thing to put in that building, and the industry is three years out from a strike where this exact question was the fight, not a footnote.

Nobody has to think Belsky is lying to think the incentive is now pointing somewhere it was not pointing before.

## Our hands are not clean

We are not writing this from a high place. Interview 002 shipped with an AI illustration of its subject on the card. It was good. It looked like him. It sat on this site for four days.

We took it down this morning and put up a frame from the actual sit down, shot on the A camera, of the actual man at his actual station. It is a better picture and it was always available. We used the generated one because it was faster, and faster is exactly the reason anybody uses it, including a studio with seventy five million new dollars and a storyboard department.

The rule here now is simple and not very brave, because we already broke it once. If a person is real and we were in the room, the picture is the room.

Storyboards are the soft opening. They always are.

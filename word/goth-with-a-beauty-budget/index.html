THE LOOK

Goth With a Beauty Budget

Pinterest put a name on the look and a number on the name. Here is what happens to a subculture when the forecast finds it.
0TP-007  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  AUG 25, 2026
D. H. Friston, “Carmilla,” The Dark Blue, 1872. Public domain.

Pinterest Predicts just put a name on the look: Vamp Romantic. Their own numbers say gothic coffin nails are up 180 percent, dark romantic makeup up 160, vampire beauty up 90, and something they are calling the carmilla aesthetic up 60. They frame it as the polished cousin of goth, pulling from Victorian romance and old Hollywood instead of post punk.

First, respect where it is due: whoever at Pinterest snuck the word carmilla into a corporate trend deck is one of us and should blink twice if they need extraction.

What actually happens now

The instinct is to yell theft, and the instinct is wrong. Nobody stole anything. Carmilla is a novella from 1872. It was drinking blood a quarter century before Dracula showed up and took the credit. The look survived being buried for 150 years, it will survive being a forecast.

What a trend report actually does is hand a subculture a search volume. Search volume brings product lines. Product lines bring people who found the look before they found anything underneath it. For about a year the thrift racks get picked clean and everything is called vamp something.

Carmilla is 1872. The forecast is Q4.

And then the cycle moves on, the way it always does, and what is left is everyone who was already here. A subculture is not the nails. It is the part that does not photograph, which is exactly why a mood board platform can only ever sell the surface of it.

This house has been shooting the look since before it had a growth chart. We will be shooting it after the chart goes back down. If the forecast brings a few people all the way through the surface and into the real thing, honestly, welcome. The water is cold and we like it that way.

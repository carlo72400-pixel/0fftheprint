And something they're calling the carmilla aesthetic, up 60. They frame it as goth's polished cousin, the one that pulls from Victorian romance and old Hollywood instead of post punk.

Respect where it's due. Whoever at Pinterest snuck the word carmilla into a corporate trend deck is one of us. Blink twice if you need extraction.

## What actually happens now

The instinct is to yell theft, and the instinct's wrong. Nobody stole anything. Carmilla's a novella from 1872. It was drinking blood a quarter century before Dracula showed up and took the credit. The look survived being buried for 150 years. It'll survive being a forecast.

What a trend report actually does is hand a subculture a search volume. Search volume brings product lines. Product lines bring people who found the look before they found anything underneath it. So for about a year the thrift racks get picked clean and everything's called vamp something.

> Carmilla is 1872. The forecast is Q4.

But the cycle moves on, like it always does, and what's left is everyone who was already here. A subculture is not the nails. It's the part that doesn't photograph. That's exactly why a mood board platform can only ever sell the surface of it.

We've been shooting the look since before it had a growth chart. We'll still be shooting it after the chart goes back down. If the forecast brings a few people all the way through the surface and into the real thing, welcome. The water's cold and we like it that way.

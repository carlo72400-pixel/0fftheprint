> Spotted: a one page liability waiver on a movie's website.

We haven't seen anybody else write about it. So here.

Start with what you already know. On the weekend of September 18, a teaser ran in theaters ahead of Resident Evil. It looked like security camera footage from inside a Halloween haunt. That Monday, A24 put a 42 second cut online. A24 invites you, it says. This fall. Then a title, Slayground, and an address.

## Go to the address

The address is [runlikehell.com](https://www.runlikehell.com/), and there's a clock on it. We read the site's own code. The clock runs out Tuesday, October 20 at 9 in the morning Eastern. That's 8 o'clock in San Antonio. If you read Monday the 19th somewhere, the code says Tuesday.

The posters dropped October 1, and they stopped pretending to be for a movie. They call it a haunted house with no exit, and they tell you to run like hell. No date. No credits.

Scroll down to the privacy policy and the terms at the bottom of the site. There's a link there now that says Waiver.

## Read the waiver

One page. The file says it was made September 30. It reads like the form a real haunt hands you at the door. Fear, fainting, seizure, heart attack, all of it yours to assume. It tells you to enter at your own risk.

It also says Slayground operates under Oregon law. Oregon's where Saulnier set Green Room, in the woods outside Portland. A24 put that one out too.

So is it a prop, or is it paperwork for a door you can actually walk through? A24 hasn't said.

## But here's what nobody's confirmed

The movie's real. Deadline announced it in June 2025 under the title October, written and directed by Jeremy Saulnier, the guy who made Green Room and Rebel Ridge. Cory Michael Smith leads. Imogen Poots, Matty Matheson, Stephen Root, Young Mazino and James Badge Dale are in it.

Past that, it's all noise until A24 talks. There's no release date. The teaser says this fall and stops. There's no official plot. That Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross score you keep hearing about? It traces back to one line on World of Reel, where the writer said he'd heard it.

So nobody outside the building knows what happens when the clock hits zero. A trailer, a date or a door.

## Why we're watching

Saulnier brought Green Room to Fantastic Fest in Austin in 2015. The photo at the top of this page is from that week. On Sunday, Beyond Fest had him booked in Los Angeles for the film's tenth anniversary. If he said one word about Slayground from that stage, we haven't seen it in print.

Eleven years after Austin, he's selling a movie as a place you can walk into, in October, in a state where people line up to get chased through a warehouse.

So set an alarm.

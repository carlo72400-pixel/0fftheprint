The listing said dress to disturb, nuns, priests, angels, demons or fetish, ten dollars ahead of time and fifteen at the door, and the room took it as a deadline. Veils and clerical collars, corsets and harnesses, devil horns, a rabbit mask, one man in a full feathered black cloak. A white throne against a pink curtain that half the room sat on at some point. Purple Moss Vintage had a table of collars, magazines and leather by the wall. BAPTISM ran at The Deco on Fredericksburg Road from half past nine until the room emptied at one, billed as darkwave, industrial, aggrotech, futurepop and techno, and it was lit almost entirely in red.

## Red is the whole problem

A room lit by red LED is nearly a single channel. Green and blue arrive at a fraction of the red, so a camera has almost no colour left to describe a face with, and every automatic tool in the chain goes stupid at once: the white balance chases a wall, the face detector finds nothing, the exposure meter reads a bright wall and darkens the person standing in front of it.

So none of those ran on their own. Every frame was developed from its RAW file, not from the camera's own JPEG, on a tone curve fitted from this card's own matched pairs and applied hue true, which is the part that took the longest to get right. An earlier version of the same chain turned every red room on this card hot pink. White balance was only read off something that should be white on the subject, a white tee, the whites of eyes, and if the subject had nothing neutral on them the frame was left as shot. A gel is never corrected. The room's light is the picture.

Exposure moved on seventeen frames out of the whole night. A face lit by flash is not underexposed because the skin is dark, a silhouette is a silhouette on purpose, and a red room is supposed to look like a red room at one in the morning.

## Two versions of every photograph

01 NATURAL is the corrected set: lens, white balance, exposure, colour, tone, then a light pass in Photoshop on real layers. A soft burn around the subject, a temperature push behind them, a glow on the bulbs and the neon, a face lift where a face was lost in the haze, and a facial retouch that is deliberately slight. The first pass ran that retouch at double strength and wiped pores, freckles and a nose stud off people's faces. It was measured, caught and every portrait was finished again at half.

02 RELIQUARY is the creative one and it is built like a print, not a filter. A silver gelatin plate first, scene aware, so that under a red gel it prints through a red filter and skin comes back luminous instead of muddy. Then three inks laid back over the silver: blood crimson on everything the red rooms owned, a wine rose on the magenta haze and the throne drapes, lapis on the blue light. Faces the flash reached print silver with a whisper of rose and keep an inked mouth, because a hand tinted print tints the lips first. A face lit only by the room stays in the ink, because that is the room.

The inks do not live in the colour lookup table. They were tried there first and the keys rode the sensor noise: on a flash lit cheek with a little LED spill the crimson printed as a rash. So the keys are solved on a colour denoised copy, feathered, eased off flash lit faces, and laid in Photoshop as multiply fills through smooth masks. An ink is a plate, and a plate gets cut by hand.

Crosses upright, veils on, nothing inverted. Devotional, not desecrated. The night was in costume and the print is in costume with it.

A student walks into a library and will not stop making noise. Two strangers already sitting there decide, without saying anything, to do something about it. That is the entire film.

VOLUME is a three minute silent comedy shot in a reading room at Trinity. Nobody in it speaks. Every problem in it gets settled with a look, a raised eyebrow, and eventually a finger held up to the lips.

## The joke is the ending

The new kid drops her bag, opens a loud bag of chips, and plays a video out loud with the volume all the way up. The two quiet ones teach her how a library works, entirely by gesture, and she learns it. Then they start enjoying each other's company a little too much, and she is the one who turns around and shushes them.

That flip is the reason the film exists. Everything before it is setup.

## One person made it and is also in it

The desk wrote it, directed it, cut it, and plays one of the two quiet ones, which is a sentence that sounds better than the shoot actually was. Every shot he appears in was locked off on a tripod and left running. He only got to operate the camera on shots he is not in.

That constraint is all over the finished thing. When you cannot move the camera you compose instead, so the film ended up centred and symmetrical, which is the good version of being stuck.

The colour is a per clip pipeline scripted in Resolve rather than one preset dropped across a timeline. Pastel pink, warm tungsten, everything squared up.

## What the course director said

It picked up the Course Director's Award for the term at Full Sail. That is a course level award and we are not going to dress it up as a festival, but his read on it is the part worth repeating: that professors lecture for hours on what this said in three minutes, and that the whole trick was getting in, getting out, and making people smile.

A student walks into a library and won't stop making noise. Two strangers already sitting there decide, without saying a word, to do something about it. That's the whole film.

VOLUME is a three minute silent comedy shot in a reading room at Trinity. Nobody in it talks. Every problem in it gets settled with a look, a raised eyebrow, and eventually a finger held up to the lips.

## The joke is the ending

The new kid drops her bag, opens a loud bag of chips, and plays a video out loud with the volume all the way up. So the two quiet ones teach her how a library works, all by gesture, and she gets it. But then they start enjoying each other's company a little too much, and she's the one who turns around and shushes them.

That flip is why the film exists. Everything before it is setup.

## One person made it and he's also in it

The desk wrote it, directed it, cut it, and plays one of the two quiet ones. That's a sentence that sounds better than the shoot actually was. Every shot he's in was locked off on a tripod and left running. He only got to run the camera on shots he's not in.

That constraint is all over the finished thing. If you can't move the camera, you compose instead. So the film ended up centered and symmetrical, which is the good version of being stuck.

The color is a per clip pipeline scripted in Resolve, instead of one preset dropped across a timeline. Pastel pink, warm tungsten, everything squared up.

## What the course director said

It picked up the Course Director's Award for the term at Full Sail. That's a course level award, and we won't dress it up as a festival. But his read on it is worth repeating. His point was that professors lecture for hours on what this said in three minutes, and that the whole trick was getting in, getting out, and making people smile.

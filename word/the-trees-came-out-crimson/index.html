THE NIGHT

The Trees Came Out Crimson

Third Friday at St. Paul Square, printed twice: once as it looked, once on colour infrared where every tree in the square goes red.
0TP-024  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  SEP 19, 2026

Third Friday at Zen Haus is a street market with a DJ on a balcony, and the first thing worth saying is where it actually is. It's in Historic St. Paul Square, on the east side of downtown. It's not in La Villita, which is where half the internet will tell you it happens. The street sign's in frame 058 of this set if anybody wants to argue about it.

Doors at 7, kind of. Free entry, third Friday of the month, 40 to 50 vendors along the brick. Vintage racks out on the sidewalk, jewelry under glass, candles with jokes printed on them. A piano pushed up against a storefront window. A barbecue running at the end of the row. The Tower of the Americas sits over the rooftops in about a fifth of the frames, because there's no angle here that avoids it. Overcast dusk for the first hour, then string lights, then flash.

An all ages market is a different photo problem

A club gives you one lighting situation for four hours. A market at dusk gives you five in 20 minutes. Open sky. Shade under a balcony. Tungsten storefronts. A magenta LED room. A blue one. The frames carry all of it. On 13 of them, the camera had already applied its own local tone mapping, a bright sky held over a dark market. It's the same trick your phone pulls when the sky and the street both somehow look right. No single curve can match that, so we gave those a local tone map in Photoshop instead of a global one.

We developed everything from the RAW files. The camera stores its wide shots at full sensor width, but the JPEG carries the digital zoom. So 16 frames here are a center crop back to the framing that was actually shot, at native pixels, never an upscale. We corrected the far corners on three wide daylight frames, per channel. The lens correction this camera ships is thin out there, and it shows on an overcast sky, which is the only subject in the world that shows it.

Two versions of every photo

01 NATURAL is the corrected set, printed on a matte uncoated paper. A soft fiber tooth. A paper white just under pure white. An ink black just above zero. It's the market as it looked.

02 HAUS INFRARED is the creative one. It's color infrared film thinking, not a filter. Two inks. The first turns every tree in the square crimson, the same family as the salmon brick they stand against. The crepe myrtles go with them, and so does the AstroTurf print on the DJ's setup. The second takes every cool pixel that isn't a gel and pushes it to the cobalt off Zen Haus's own sticker and banner. That's the dusk sky, the night sky, denim, the ironwork on the stairs, the shade. Skin, blonde hair, tungsten wood, bulbs and the candle flames get keyed out and stay themselves, because a person under infrared film isn't the joke. The foliage is.

We put back green dyed hair and green logos on people by hand in Photoshop, on 28 frames. The natural color goes in on a color blend, inside the person, so somebody's hair doesn't get eaten by the same rule that turns the trees.

On the night frames, the foliage key found sensor noise instead of leaves and printed a crimson rash on a gray concrete pillar under string lights. The fix is the same one the other gallery this week needed. We solved the look a second time on a color denoised copy and laid it in only where the two solutions disagree, which is exactly where the key was unstable. Real foliage keys the same in both, so every leaf survives.

Where it lives

The cleared frames from Third Friday are up, both versions of each, full quality, free to download. If you were shopping, selling, DJing or just standing under the lights that evening, you're probably in one of them.

THE NIGHT

The Trees Came Out Crimson

Third Friday at St. Paul Square, printed twice: once as it looked, once on colour infrared where every tree in the square goes red.
0TP-024  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  SEP 19, 2026

Third Friday at Zen Haus is a street market with a DJ on a balcony, and the first thing worth saying about it is where it is. It is in Historic St. Paul Square on the east side of downtown, not in La Villita, which is where half the internet will tell you it happens. The street sign is in frame 058 of this set if anybody wants to argue about it.

Doors of a sort at seven, free entry, third Friday of the month, forty to fifty vendors along the brick. Vintage racks out on the sidewalk, jewellery under glass, candles with jokes printed on them, a piano pushed up against a shopfront window, a barbecue running at the end of the row. The Tower of the Americas sits over the rooftops in about a fifth of the frames because there is no angle here that avoids it. Overcast dusk for the first hour, then string lights, then flash.

An all ages market is a different photography problem

A club gives you one lighting situation for four hours. A market at dusk gives you five in twenty minutes: open sky, shade under a balcony, tungsten shopfronts, a magenta LED room, a blue one. The frames carry it. Thirteen of them came off the camera with its own local tone mapping already applied, a bright sky held over a dark market, which no single curve can match, so those got a local tone map in Photoshop instead of a global one.

Everything was developed from the RAW files. The camera stores its wide shots at full sensor width while the JPEG carries the digital zoom, so sixteen frames here are a centre crop back to the framing that was actually shot, at native pixels, never an upscale. Three wide daylight frames had their far corners corrected per channel: the lens correction this camera ships is thin out there and it shows on an overcast sky, which is the only subject in the world that shows it.

Two versions of every photograph

01 NATURAL is the corrected set, printed on a matte uncoated paper: a soft fibre tooth, a paper white just under pure white, an ink black just above zero. It is the market as it looked.

02 HAUS INFRARED is the creative one and it is colour infrared film thinking, not a filter. Two inks. The first turns every tree in the square crimson, the crepe myrtles and the AstroTurf print on the DJ's setup with them, into the same family as the salmon brick they stand against. The second takes every cool pixel that is not a gel, the dusk sky, the night sky, denim, the ironwork on the stairs, the shade, and pushes it to the cobalt off Zen Haus's own sticker and banner. Skin, blonde hair, tungsten wood, bulbs and the candle flames are keyed out and stay themselves, because a person under infrared film is not the joke, the foliage is.

Green dyed hair and green logos on people are put back by hand in Photoshop on twenty eight frames, the natural colour laid in colour blend inside the person, so somebody's hair does not get eaten by the same rule that turns the trees.

On the night frames the foliage key found sensor noise instead of leaves and printed a crimson rash on a grey concrete pillar under string lights. The fix is the same one the other gallery this week needed: solve the look a second time on a colour denoised copy and lay it in only where the two solutions disagree, which is exactly where the key was unstable. Real foliage keys the same in both, so every leaf survives.

Where it lives

The cleared frames from Third Friday, both versions of each, full quality, free to download. If you were shopping, selling, DJing or just standing under the lights that evening, you are probably in one of them.

Zen Haus, 119 Heiman Street, Historic St. Paul Square, San Antonio. Friday 18 September 2026, seven until nine.

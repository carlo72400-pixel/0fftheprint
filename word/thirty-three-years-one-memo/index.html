Thirty-Three Years, One Memo

Jensen Huang told his staff he cheated. US officials say the woman was recruited by China's spy agency. China calls it a smear. By Thursday he was out.
0TP-058  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  NOV 12, 2026

Jensen Huang at SC18 in Dallas on November 12, 2018, by Raysonho @ Open Grid Scheduler / Scalable Grid Engine, via Wikimedia Commons, CC0. Cropped and restyled.

Spotted: an SEC filing that says Nvidia's CEO is out, effective immediately.

You know him from the keynotes. The leather jacket, a graphics card in his hand. As of Thursday he doesn't run the company.

Here's the week

Sunday, November 8, Nvidia put a message from Jensen Huang on its own newsroom. He told his staff he'd been unfaithful to his wife and said sorry. He backed the board's review and asked for privacy for his family. The memo doesn't name anybody.

The rest came from The New York Times. It reported he told the board he'd had a relationship with a Chinese citizen who was a senior executive at Megaspeed, a company in Singapore that bought Nvidia chips. And it reported US officials concluded China's spy agency recruited her.

Monday the stock closed down about 4.3 percent, roughly $190 billion. That day Trump said the FBI is looking into it, and that Huang told him on the phone he had no idea she was a spy.

Thursday, before the market opened, the filing hit. He's out as president, CEO and director. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Harvey Jones, on the board since 1993, is the first chair Nvidia's ever had.

But read the word alleged

Nobody's been charged with anything. China's foreign ministry called the spy claim unsubstantiated and a smear, twice. On November 8 the board said it had no reason to believe Nvidia's secrets or US national security were compromised. NBC confirmed the intelligence memo with its own sources, and one of them said she may not have been a career spy at all.

So the affair's on the record because he put it there. The spy part is what officials concluded, and it stays alleged until somebody proves it.

What you heard and what's on the record

You heard he got fired. The filing says he resigned and the board accepted it. Neither the filing nor the release gives a reason. A spokesperson told NBC he won't work with Nvidia in any capacity.

You heard it's over. It isn't. House committee chairs want a closed-door interview on December 1, and Senate Banking wants him December 3. Shareholders already sued, Monday, in federal court in San Jose. And Thursday, Trump posted that Tim Cook should take the job. Cook already has one. He's Apple's executive chair.

So here's the Texas part

In July, Huang was on a stage in Fort Worth, opening the Wistron plant that builds Nvidia's supercomputers. Foxconn's plant in Houston was still going up as of August. Nvidia announced both in April 2025.

Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday, November 17, around 3:20 PM San Antonio time. It's the first call without him.

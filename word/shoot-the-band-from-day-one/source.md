> Spotted: a band doc that starts on an unfinished demo, not on the reunion.

Most music documentaries wait until the story is safe. This one started before the first chorus was over.

## Start at the song

Texas Standard, November 2. Andrew Morgan directed Bleeding Hearts. A friend in Nashville sent him an unfinished demo of the band's first song, also called Bleeding Hearts. He told Texas Standard it got him before he'd finished the first chorus. The friend said these people were starting a relationship and starting a band. Morgan pitched a film about the start of a band.

He told Texas Standard the usual music doc looks back from decades later, and he wanted to follow "this beautiful, fragile thing right from day one."

That's the note if you shoot bands. The legend is the easy film. Day one doesn't know if the band survives the year. You don't get a reunion speech. You get a demo that isn't finished, and you roll anyway.

## Who's on the bill

Austin Film Festival's first-wave post, August 26. Bleeding Hearts is a Texas premiere. The cast is Zella Day, Jesse Woods and Beau Bedford. The synopsis calls it a music-driven love story about Zella Day and Jesse Woods, the couple behind the band Chaparelle, following their first year. The joy, the uncertainty, and the pressure that could be a breakup or a breakthrough.

Texas Standard's shorter version: a Texas band on the rise.

The same AFF post says Chaparelle will play an intimate set after the screening. That's a billing, from August. Texas Standard on November 2 doesn't mention the set, and screenings were still going through Thursday. We can't tell you the set already happened. We can tell you the festival put the band in the room after the film. That's the opposite of a decades-later doc. The subject isn't a memory. The subject is on the bill.

## The festival around it

AFF runs October 29 to November 5. It's the 33rd. The film side screens more than 150 films across eight days. The writers conference is four days. Texas Standard's point about the fest is that it highlights the script, so some of the biggest names in town weren't the stars.

This one started from a demo, not a script. The song showed up unfinished. If you shoot bands, that's the argument. Roll while the thing is still fragile. Cut the movie after. A writers' festival can still premiere it.

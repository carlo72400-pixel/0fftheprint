THE FESTIVAL

The Blood Countess Is Playing Austin

Fantastic Fest runs September 17 to 24 on South Lamar. Isabelle Huppert plays Elizabeth Bathory, a vampire Santa gets a world premiere, and GWAR closes the whole thing.
0TP-015  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  SEP 02, 2026

Robert Eggers at Fantastic Fest 2015, the year The Witch played there, by anna Hanks, via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. Cropped.

Fantastic Fest runs September 17 to 24 at the Alamo Drafthouse on South Lamar. That's 80 miles up I-35. It's the 21st edition. The program is 35 world premieres, 21 international and North American premieres, and 14 US premieres. That makes it one of the largest genre festivals on the continent, and the closest one to this city by a wide margin.

Most years you could call the slate horror and leave it there. But this year it's specifically our shelf.

Isabelle Huppert is Elizabeth Bathory

The Blood Countess is Ulrike Ottinger's film, and it's got the North American premiere slot. Huppert plays the Hungarian noblewoman who's been the load bearing wall of vampire folklore for 400 years without ever actually being a vampire.

That casting is the whole reason to pay attention. Bathory usually gets handed to a genre studio and comes back as a bathing scene. Ottinger's an experimental filmmaker in her eighties. Huppert doesn't take a part to be looked at.

The rest of the shelf

Victorian Psycho takes another North American premiere, directed by Zachary Wigon with Maika Monroe in it. She's spent a decade being the most reliable face in American horror.

Red Snow is a world premiere. The logline is a vampire posing as Santa Claus who picks the wrong house, because the teenage daughter inside wants to be turned.

Mike Flanagan's Carrie takes a US premiere. Zach Cregger's Resident Evil gets a gala screening. Mike P. Nelson's Beware Boiuna opens the whole thing.

It closes with GWAR

The last film is The Swallow, from Kevin Kolsch and Dennis Widmyer. Then there's a closing night concert from GWAR. A film festival that ends with gore rock knows exactly who's in the room.

Why this one and not the others

Robert Eggers brought The Witch to Fantastic Fest in 2015. The photo at the top of this page is from that week. He's in a director's chair against the step and repeat, a year before most people knew the name.

That's the argument for driving up. This isn't a festival that programs prestige horror after it's been anointed somewhere else. It's one of the rooms where the anointing happens, and it happens an hour and a half from here, in a theater that'll serve you a milkshake while a vampire Santa kills a family.

Tickets and the full program are at fantasticfest.com.

THE FESTIVAL

The Blood Countess Is Playing Austin

Fantastic Fest runs September 17 to 24 on South Lamar. Isabelle Huppert plays Elizabeth Bathory, a vampire Santa gets a world premiere, and GWAR closes the whole thing.
0TP-015  ·  0FF THE PRINT  ·  San Antonio  ·  SEP 02, 2026

Robert Eggers at Fantastic Fest 2015, the year The Witch played there, by anna Hanks, via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. Cropped.

Fantastic Fest runs September 17 to 24 at the Alamo Drafthouse on South Lamar. That is eighty miles up I-35. It is the twenty first edition and the programme is 35 world premieres, 21 international and North American premieres, and 14 US premieres, which makes it one of the largest genre festivals on the continent and the closest one to this city by a wide margin.

Most years you could describe the slate as horror and leave it there. This year it is specifically our shelf.

Isabelle Huppert is playing Elizabeth Bathory

The Blood Countess is Ulrike Ottinger's film, it takes the North American premiere slot, and Huppert plays the Hungarian noblewoman who has been the load bearing wall of vampire folklore for four hundred years without ever actually being a vampire.

That casting is the whole reason to pay attention. Bathory usually gets handed to a genre studio and comes back as a bathing scene. Ottinger is an experimental film maker in her eighties and Huppert does not take a part to be looked at.

And it keeps going

Victorian Psycho takes another North American premiere, directed by Zachary Wigon with Maika Monroe in it, who has spent a decade being the most reliable face in American horror.

Red Snow is a world premiere and the logline is a vampire posing as Santa Claus who picks the wrong house, because the teenage daughter inside wants to be turned.

Mike Flanagan's Carrie takes a US premiere. Zach Cregger's Resident Evil gets a gala screening. Mike P. Nelson's Beware Boiuna opens the whole thing.

It closes with GWAR

The last film is The Swallow, from Kevin Kolsch and Dennis Widmyer, and then a closing night concert from GWAR. A film festival that ends with gore rock is a festival that knows exactly who is in the room.

Why this one and not the others

Robert Eggers premiered The Witch at Fantastic Fest in 2015. The photograph at the top of this page is from that week, him in a director's chair against the step and repeat, a year before most people knew the name.

That is the argument for driving up. This is not a festival that programmes prestige horror after it has been anointed somewhere else. It is one of the rooms where the anointing happens, and it happens an hour and a half from here, in a cinema that will serve you a milkshake while a vampire Santa kills a family.

Tickets and the full programme are at fantasticfest.com.

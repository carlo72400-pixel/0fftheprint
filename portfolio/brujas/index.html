0ff The Print / Interview 03

ThePage

Angel · @avocado__papi
Creator of BRUJAS · La Llorona, La Lechuza, the Donkey Lady · San Antonio

What it is

You drawing, start to finish, talking while you work. Four to six minutes. The pen is moving the whole time.

I come to wherever you actually draw. Nothing gets staged, nothing gets cleaned up for me, and you don't have to perform. You work on a page, I ask you about Brujas, the camera stays out of your light.

  • Not a talking head against a wall
  • Not a portfolio reel with music over it
  • Not scripted, rehearsed, or done in takes

How the day runs

Three hours at your desk

  1. Before you sit down

    The desk empty

    I shoot your setup with nobody in it. Your tools, your mess, the wall behind you, whatever's taped up. This is the opening.

  2. Paper, two minutes

    Signatures

    You sign a release, and so does whoever owns the space. It says I can film you and the work. It does not touch who owns the art. That stays yours, all of it.

  3. Blank page

    First frame

    Empty page, pen not down yet. Quiet, no talking. That's the shot the whole thing opens on.

  4. Pen down · about 2 hours

    The conversation

    This is the whole interview and you're drawing through all of it. If you need to shut up and focus, shut up and focus. Silence with the pen scratching is good footage, not dead air.

  5. Wherever you get to

    You will not finish, that's fine

    Nobody finishes a page in three hours and I'm not expecting one. Getting a panel from nothing to inked is more than enough to build a piece on.

  6. Last 5 minutes

    Just you

    You holding the page, and stills of you at the desk. Those are the photos that go on the site and on your card.

Where the cameras sit

Two, and neither one is in your way

No lights in your face. Whatever your desk lamp is doing is the look. The only thing I care about is that the page reads clean.

What it sounds like

The pen is the soundtrack

No music over your voice, no narrator, no questions read off a card. It's a conversation while you draw. Three of the kind of things I'll ask, so you know what you're walking into:

  • The Donkey Lady is a San Antonio story. Did you grow up on the bridge?
  • You made them friends instead of monsters. Where did that turn come from?
  • What do you draw when nobody's paying and nobody's watching?
House rules Anything you don't want in it, say so and it's gone, even if it's already on camera. Anything unpublished you don't want seen, tell me and it stays out of frame. Off the record stays off the record, that's a real rule here, not a line.

The trade

What you get, what I need

You get

  • The finished piece, 4 to 6 minutes
  • Three vertical cuts for your page
  • Process clips and stills of the art
  • All of it before it goes anywhere public
  • No charge, ever, for any of it

I need

  • About three hours at your desk
  • A page you were going to draw anyway
  • To know if you work on paper or a tablet
  • The ok from whoever owns the space

## 1. Rate benchmarks (US, Texas, San Antonio and Austin, 2024–2026) compared with the existing rate card

### Takeaway
The benchmarks are mostly vendor and marketplace pages, and very few are Texas-specific. Against them, the rate card sits in four places:
- **At or below the market floor:** edited event video with 24-hour turnaround (Event Recap $600), and especially bundled photo + video coverage (Event Full $900).
- **Roughly in line:** social content days and monthly retainers.
- **Low end of the range:** independent music videos.
- **Mid-market:** editing and color hourly rates.

The biggest structural gap is that the card prices no usage or licensing, and every 2025–2026 pricing guide found treats usage as its own line item.

## 2. Licensing concert and event images and footage

### Takeaway
Licensing is priced by **use**. The dividing lines are editorial vs. commercial, organic social vs. paid ads, and merch/print vs. web, and sources consistently advise keeping usage separate from the shooting fee. Concert-photo licensing benchmarks are thin and dated:
- about $100–$200 per image for publication use (2017)
- about $100+ per image floor for merch (undated)

A common pattern is free, low-res, credited reposting for performers and venues, with paid ads, merch and sponsor use licensed separately.

### Cited Findings
- **Editorial vs. commercial (ASMP):**
  - Editorial and fine-art uses typically don't need releases; commercial uses do.
  - Self-promotion counts as commercial use, including your homepage, direct mail and portfolio.
  - Covers, or a publication's reuse of its own editorial images to market itself, can be classed as commercial.
  - Publication contracts may require releases even when the law doesn't.
  - Source: [ASMP, "Model Releases – Questions and Answers"](https://www.asmp.org/strictly-business-blog/model-releases-questions-answers/)
- **Festival-style tiered terms:** one festival lets performers promote with its photos if they credit the photographer and festival. Merchandise or paid ads require a commercial license. — [Moisture Festival photo/video terms](https://moisturefestival.org/?p=10402)
- **Concert-photo service tiers:** a Seattle-area concert photographer offers two tiers:
  - Web-only: low-resolution files that must keep a copyright stamp and a link.
  - Broader license for magazines, labels and publicists.
  - Source: [Grave Music / Mortado services](https://www.mortado.com/gravemusic/metalphoto/services.shtml)
- **Venue credential terms:** one venue's media terms promise photo credit only "where reasonably practical" and make a marketing license a condition of access, while the photographer keeps copyright. Read credential terms before shooting. — [Erie Events media requests policy](https://www.erieevents.com/about/media-requests)
- **Photo-pass releases:** these often confine use to the outlet named on the application, so check them before posting or licensing. Per search extract; overlaps with the credentials researcher. — [iShootShows, "Copyright and Concert Photography"](https://ishootshows.com/copyright-and-concert-photography/)
- **Per-image prices (old or anecdotal):**
  - Publication license $100–$200 per image (2017). — [PetaPixel 2017](https://petapixel.com/2017/02/08/concert-photography-jobs-make-money-shooting-music/)
  - Merch and poster use about $100+ per photo, sometimes with a merch-revenue share. — [Showgraphers](https://www.showgraphers.com/faqs-musicians)
  - A forum poster suggested a one-year unlimited-use license of roughly $300–$500 for an up-and-coming act, while acknowledging others may know better. This is anecdote; attribution is per a search extract of a dgrin thread. — [dgrin forum](https://dgrin.com/discussion/127342/rock-n-roll-photography)
- **Sponsor use:**
  - In a widely covered dispute, a band's sponsor used a concert photo to promote merch. The photographer asked for a €100 license fee payable to charity, and the band sided with the sponsor ("exposure").
  - Techdirt's commentary noted the band had little say over a partner's use of photos the photographer owned.
  - Source: [Techdirt, Arch Enemy tag](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/arch-enemy/)
- **"Free for exposure" requests:**
  - A UK survey covering 2016 found 87% of freelance photographers were asked to work free and 16% agreed (2017). — [PetaPixel (Jan 2017)](https://petapixel.com/2017/01/23/87-uk-freelance-photogs-asked-work-free-2016-16-said-yes)
  - Tony Wu's reusable reply letter notes the photographer is often the only unpaid party on a project (2011). — [PetaPixel (2011)](https://petapixel.com/2011/10/13/how-to-respond-to-requests-for-free-photography/)
  - Fstoppers advises a calm reply, treating unpaid work as off the table, and offering a comparable-value trade instead. — [Fstoppers, "3 Tips on How to Turn Exposure Into Actual Money"](https://fstoppers.com/business/3-tips-how-turn-exposure-actual-money-137932)
- **Paid social and ads are a separate fee:**
  - Later (2026) says whitelisting costs extra. — [Later](https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/)
  - Idukki's sample charges an additional $120 for one year of paid-ads rights on a $90 organic video. — [Idukki](https://idukki.io/blog/ugc-creator-rate-card-what-to-charge.md)
- **Usage as its own line:** Artfolio (2025) treats usage rights as a separate event-video cost component. — [Artfolio](https://www.artfolio.com/article/pricing-event-videography-in-transparent-quotes-that-win-contracts)

  - A 1995 Comptroller letter (via Ezel summary) treated a fee for the right to publish a copyrighted image as nontaxable. Hired photos delivered to an ad agency were taxable unless the agency gave a resale certificate.
  - The letter is old and fact-specific; see Q7.

### Inferences
- **Suggested license ladder for this shooter's free public galleries and client work.** This synthesizes the festival, concert-service and venue examples above; it is not an industry standard.
  1. **Personal and fan sharing:** free, with credit or tag.
  2. **Artist, venue or promoter organic social repost** (their own feeds and stories): free or included in paid coverage. Conditions: mandatory photo credit and handle tag; no cropping out of credit; no alteration beyond cropping.
  3. **Paid promotion** (boosted posts, ads, whitelisting, ticketing-platform ads): a separate fee per term (e.g., 6 or 12 months) and per platform.
  4. **Merch, posters and album art:** a per-image license (floor about $100 per Showgraphers; more for a pressed release or large print runs).
  5. **Press, editorial or label publicity:** per image (old benchmark $100–$200) or a press-kit bundle.
  6. **Sponsor or third-party brand use:** never included. Licenses should be **non-transferable and non-sublicensable** to sponsors without a separate fee (the lesson of the Arch Enemy dispute).
- For **footage**, the same ladder applies: an organic social license is included in the package; ads, broadcast or paid use require extension. Raw footage should come with a license limiting re-editing for paid or commercial use unless that use is purchased (see Q4).
- **Credit norms are not standardized.** The sources show credit as a condition of free use (festival, concert service), but venues may promise credit only "where practical." Writing "credit required: Photo: [Name] / @handle" into every license is the practical lever. **[Inference]**
- **Gallery use and releases.** Because the shooter publishes free galleries, letting a venue or promoter use gallery images featuring **identifiable patrons in ads** raises release issues (Q5). Licenses for advertising use should state that no model releases were obtained and that the licensee is responsible for securing any needed releases. **[Legal interpretation; verify with counsel]**

- No current published fee data for licensing to labels, promoters or press specifically in Texas.
- ASMP's own licensing and pricing guides could not be fetched (asmp.org blocked).

## 3. Copyright: default ownership, registration (group options, fees, timing) and handling infringement

### Takeaway
The photographer or videographer owns copyright by default. "Work made for hire" (WMFH) is narrow for commissioned stills, but a signed WMFH clause can work for **video**, because "part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work" is a statutory category.

Registration and enforcement in brief:
- **Registering galleries:** register published galleries with GRPPH (up to 750 photos, one fee, same calendar year) **within three months of publication** to keep statutory damages and attorney's fees available.
- **Current fees:** $55 group, $45 single, $65 standard.
- **Proposed fees:** $85 group, $55 single, $85 standard. They were submitted to Congress on July 14, 2026 and can take effect no earlier than about **Nov 11, 2026**.
- **Small claims:** the Copyright Claims Board (CCB) is a $100, two-part-fee route capped at $30,000.

### Cited Findings

**Default ownership and work made for hire**
- The Copyright Office treats the person who takes a photo as its default author; WMFH is a narrow exception. — [Copyright Office, "What Photographers Should Know about Copyright"](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/photographers)
- **Circular 30** recognizes two WMFH paths:
  - Work an employee creates within the scope of the job.
  - Certain commissioned works made under an express written agreement signed by both parties.
  - Source: [Copyright Office Circular 30 (PDF)](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf); requirements as summarized at [Craftshift](https://craftshift.com/who-owns-shopify-product-photos-copyright/)
- The nine eligible commissioned categories are:
  - contributions to collective works
  - **parts of motion pictures or other audiovisual works**
  - translations
  - supplementary works
  - compilations
  - instructional texts
  - tests
  - answer material for tests
  - atlases
  - Photography as such is not listed. — [LegalZoom WMFH glossary](https://www.legalzoom.com/business-glossary/work-made-for-hire); [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/?p=2695)
- Employee status is decided under common-law agency factors (CCNV v. Reid, 1989). A freelance photo in *Foster v. Lee* failed WMFH for lack of a written agreement. — [Loeb & Loeb on Foster v. Lee (2015)](https://www.loeb.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2015/03/foster-v-lee)
- Clients sometimes pair a WMFH declaration with a backup copyright **assignment** that takes effect if the WMFH label fails. — [Vanderbilt WMFH photographer agreement](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/communications/marketing-solutions/Work_for_Hire_Photographer_Agreement.pdf)
- Producers and clients of motion pictures and web video "almost always" try to keep copyright. — [Photofocus, "What is work for hire"](https://photofocus.com/business/know-your-rights-what-is-work-for-hire/)

**Registration options and fees**
- **Current fees** (Copyright Office fee page, via search extract):
  - Single Application: $45 (one work, one author who is the sole owner, not WMFH; online only).
  - Standard Application: $65 online, $125 paper.
  - An older version of the fee page shows outdated $35/$55 figures.
  - Source: [Copyright Office fees](https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html)
- **GRPPH** (Group Registration for Published Photographs) requirements:
  - Up to **750** photos per application, one fee.
  - All photos must be published in the **same calendar year**.
  - Single author and single claimant.
  - A numbered list of titles and file names, with publication dates.
  - Published and unpublished photos can't be mixed.
  - Source: [Copyright Office GRPPH help](https://copyright.gov/eco/help/group/grpph.html)
- **GRUPH** (unpublished photographs): the same 750-photo cap. — [Copyright Office GRUPH help](https://www.copyright.gov/eco/help/group/gruph.html)
- The group photo fee has been $55 since the 2020 fee rule, which raised the standard application fee but kept group photos at $55. — [Federal Register, "Copyright Office Fees" final rule (Feb 19, 2020)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/02/19/2020-03268/copyright-office-fees); [Copyright Office 2018 fee study](https://copyright.gov/rulemaking/feestudy2018/)
- **Proposed fee schedule:**
  - A notice of proposed rulemaking was published March 19, 2026 (Federal Register doc 2026-05529).
  - The final proposed schedule was submitted to Congress **July 14, 2026**. It takes effect after a 120-day review window unless Congress disapproves; that window ends about **Nov 11, 2026**.
  - Proposed: **group photos $55 → $85** (published and unpublished), **Single Application $45 → $55**, **Standard Application $65 → $85** online and $185 paper.
  - Sources: [Copyright Office Fee Study 2026](https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/feestudy2026/); [proposed fee schedule PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/feestudy2026/proposed-fee-schedule.pdf); [PetaPixel, Jul 14, 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/14/copyright-office-will-increase-photo-registration-cost-by-55-in-120-days/); [PolicyRisk, FR 2026-05529](https://policyrisk.com/federal-register/2026-05529); corroborated by [NAR (Jul 2026)](https://www.nar.realtor/news/washington-report/us-copyright-office-submits-mls-fee-increase-to-congress)
- ASMP calls the group increase "nearly 55%" and campaigned against it. NPPA objected publicly. — [ASMP advocacy](https://www.asmp.org/advocacy/the-copyright-office-wants-to-raise-registration-fees-take-this-survey-and-help-us-tell-them-no/); [PetaPixel, May 5, 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/05/05/us-copyright-office-wants-to-increase-cost-to-register-photos-by-55/)
- Fstoppers, citing the fee study, puts the Office's processing cost at $105.52 per published group and $150.77 per unpublished group. It frames the period before mid-November as a window to file at $55. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/legal/what-upcoming-copyright-office-fee-increase-means-photographers-903585)
- Digital Camera World reported that a **subscription-style** registration option "isn't yet off the table." — [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/photography/the-us-copyright-office-could-drastically-increase-fees-for-photographers-this-fall-but-a-subscription-option-isnt-yet-off-the-table)
- **Conflict:** one guide (Promise Legal) lists GRUPH at $85 "today," which appears to be an error. — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/copyright-registration-image-series-single-vs-group/)

**Timing rules for statutory damages (17 U.S.C. § 412)**
- Statutory damages and attorney's fees are available only if:
  - the work was registered **before the infringement began**, or
  - it was registered **within three months after first publication**.
- Unpublished works must be registered before the infringement. Registering within the three-month window can reach infringement that predates registration. Otherwise only actual damages and profits are available.
- Source: [Copyright Office letter to Sen. Tillis on publication (Dec 1, 2021)](https://copyright.gov/laws/hearings/Letter-on-Publication-Study-12-1-2021-FINAL.pdf); [Compendium ch. 1900](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap1900/ch1900-publication.pdf)
- The same letter acknowledges that uncertainty over whether online works are "published" can cause owners to delay and miss the window. Registration (or a refusal) is required before suing on a US work (§ 411(a)). — [Copyright Office letter (2021)](https://copyright.gov/laws/hearings/Letter-on-Publication-Study-12-1-2021-FINAL.pdf)

**Infringement: Copyright Claims Board (CCB)**
- **Filing fee:** $100 total, paid as **$40 to file** and **$60 once the case proceeds** after the opt-out period. The $60 is never due if all respondents opt out.
- **Damages caps:** $30,000 total per proceeding (fees and costs excluded), **$15,000 per work** in statutory damages, and a smaller-claims track capped at **$5,000**.
- **Comparison with federal court:** actual damages are uncapped and willful infringement can bring up to **$150,000 per work**, so CCB awards are generally lower.
- Sources: [CCB, About](https://ccb.gov/about/); [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/final-rules-set-for-new-u-s-copyright-claims-board-to-hear-small-claims/)
- Other CCB fees: expedited registration for a CCB claim is $50 per work; a Register's review of a final decision is $300. — [CCB, About (via search extract)](https://ccb.gov/about/)
- A pre-launch Duane Morris alert says fee awards depend on bad-faith conduct and are capped at $5,000. It also suggests statutory damages may be halved for works not timely registered. **Unverified** against CCB material; check 17 U.S.C. § 1504(e). — [Duane Morris (2021)](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_copyright_claims_board_may_have_limited_appeal_0121.html)

### Inferences
- **Registration workflow.** The shooter publishes nightly galleries, so a **monthly or at-least-quarterly GRPPH batch** of published images (≤750 per filing, same calendar year) would keep each batch inside the three-month window for statutory damages and fees. **[Legal interpretation]** Whether a public gallery counts as "publication" is unsettled, per the Copyright Office's own 2021 letter. A cautious approach:
  - Register unpublished selects via GRUPH before release, or
  - register galleries as published within three months.
- **Fee timing.** The increase can't take effect before about Nov 11, 2026. Filing any pending 2026 batches before then saves $30 per group filing ($55 vs. $85), assuming the schedule takes effect as proposed.
- **Video.** No group option for video turned up in this research (see Gaps). Recap edits, music videos and brand films would be registered one at a time:
  - $45 Single Application if the shooter is the sole author and owner and the work is not WMFH
  - $65 Standard Application otherwise
  - Under the proposal, $55 and $85.
  - Registration is most worthwhile for music videos and brand films where infringement or reposting risk is real.
- **Contract watch-out.** For **video** commissions (brand films, music videos, ad reels), a signed "work made for hire" clause can actually transfer authorship, because audiovisual works are a listed category. For **stills**, WMFH generally fails, but an "assignment" clause would still transfer copyright. To keep ownership, the shooter should strike or replace both with an **exclusive or non-exclusive license** of defined scope. **[Legal interpretation]**
- **Enforcement path.** Escalate in this order:
  1. Platform takedown (DMCA).
  2. Demand letter with a retroactive license invoice.
  3. CCB (≤$30k, $100 fee, respondent can opt out).
  4. Federal court (requires registration; statutory damages only if timely registered).

### Gaps
- **DMCA takedown requirements (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)) were not verified:** law.cornell.edu and copyright.gov were blocked and the search budget was exhausted. From general knowledge, **to verify**, a notice typically needs:
  - a signature

  - Platform-specific flows (Instagram/Meta, TikTok, YouTube) and counter-notice timelines were not researched.

## 4. Contracts: essential clauses for event, venue, promoter and artist work, and template sources

### Takeaway
No authoritative 2024–2026 survey of contract norms for event or concert photo + video was found. The usual recommended starting points are ASMP and APA terms and PPA member templates. The sources that were found emphasize:
- cancellation fees tied to notice
- nonrefundable retainers
- separately stated fees, expenses and payment timing
- usage priced on its own line
- written license or assignment language

Texas law supplies a **6%/yr default interest rate only when no rate is agreed**, starting 30 days after the debt is due, and **justice court (small claims) handles claims up to $20,000**.

### Cited Findings
- **Template sources:** PetaPixel's 2020 guide, written during COVID postponements, points photographers to **ASMP and APA** for terms and conditions on postponement and cancellation. — [PetaPixel (Mar 2020)](https://petapixel.com/2020/03/21/photo-assignments-postponed-take-these-6-steps-now-to-protect-yourself)
- **Cancellation patterns** in published photographer terms:
  - Cancellation charges are tied to how much notice the client gives.
  - Retainers are often nonrefundable.
  - Estimate and invoice terms spell out fees, expenses and payment timing.
  - Several examples are UK-drafted; one is governed by English law. Don't copy their interest terms for Texas use.
  - Sources: [Sue Allatt terms](https://www.sueallatt.com/terms/); [Matthew Horwood T&Cs (2022)](https://matt-horwood.com/tcs-matthew-horwood-press-pr-photography); [SARAHDAW agency T&Cs (Jan 2026 upload)](https://www.sarahdaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/SARAHDAW-AGENCY-T-Cs_.pdf)
- **Usage priced separately:** Artfolio (2025) prices usage rights and contingencies as separate quote components. — [Artfolio](https://www.artfolio.com/article/pricing-event-videography-in-transparent-quotes-that-win-contracts)
- **Licensing as its own line:** Imagen AI (Dec 2025) advises keeping licensing out of the day fee. — [Imagen AI](https://imagen-ai.com/post/the-beginners-photography-pricing-guide.md)
- **Release obligations:** publication contracts often contain release obligations beyond what the law requires (ASMP counsel). — [ASMP Q&A](https://www.asmp.org/strictly-business-blog/model-releases-questions-answers/)
- **WMFH plus backup assignment:** client-drafted agreements may include both (see Q3). — [Vanderbilt WMFH agreement](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/communications/marketing-solutions/Work_for_Hire_Photographer_Agreement.pdf)
- **Venue and credential terms** may impose a marketing license as a condition of access. — [Erie Events](https://www.erieevents.com/about/media-requests)
- **Texas default interest:** Tex. Fin. Code § 302.002 applies **6% a year** where no rate is agreed, accruing from the **30th day** after the amount is due.
  - If a rate is agreed, the default drops out and Chapter 303 (optional rate ceilings) governs.
  - A charge that functions as interest is treated as an agreed rate.
  - Sources: [Tex. Fin. Code § 302.002 (Texas.Public.Law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_302.002); [Texas State Securities Board, legal rate of interest](https://ssb.texas.gov/texas-securities-act-and-board-rules/legal-rate-interest)
- **Texas small claims:**
  - Justice courts hear civil claims up to **$20,000**, raised from $10,000 effective Sept 1, 2020.
  - Attorney fees count toward the cap; statutory interest and court costs don't.
  - The KPRC explainer (Jul 2025) still gives $20,000.
  - Sources disagree on whether a larger claim can be reduced to fit.
  - Sources: [Texas State Law Library FAQ](https://sll.texas.gov/faqs/small-claims-maximum); [KPRC (Jul 2025)](https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/local/2025/07/18/how-to-file-in-texas-small-claims-court-and-what-the-limits-are); [Nolo](https://nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/texas-small-claims-court-32055.html)

### Inferences
**Clause checklist for this shooter.** This is a synthesis; the sources support the cancellation, retainer, usage, release and WMFH points. Every item should be reviewed against ASMP, APA or PPA templates and, ideally, by a Texas attorney. **[Legal interpretation]**

1. **Parties, event and access:** date, venue, call time, coverage window, photo-pit or all-access credentials, and who arranges them.
2. **Scope and deliverables:** counts and specs, e.g.:
   - recap 60–90 s, 16:9
   - 3 or 5 verticals, 9:16
   - number of edited stills
   - resolution and format
   - whether Insta360 or 360 footage is included
3. **Turnaround:** e.g., "within 24 hours of event end." Define rush and next-day fees, and say when the clock starts and pauses (client feedback delays).
4. **Revisions:** e.g., one round included, then the hourly edit rate ($65/hr) applies.
5. **Music:** the client supplies licensed music or accepts platform-library audio. Music licensing for paid ads is the client's responsibility.
6. **Usage and license** (the core clause): media (organic social, web, paid ads, broadcast, print or merch), term, territory, exclusivity, and whether the license is **non-transferable and non-sublicensable** (no sponsor use without a fee). Copyright stays with the shooter. Strike WMFH and assignment language unless separately priced (see Q3).
7. **Credit:** required credit line and handle tag on social posts; remedy for missing credit (e.g., a fee multiplier).
8. **Shooter's retained rights:** portfolio, self-promotion and **editorial publication in the shooter's own outlet** (important given the free public galleries). Carve-outs for private or "no-publish" events.
9. **Raw footage:** whether raw footage is delivered at all, the fee, and a license (not ownership) limited to the client's own use. No resale, no use in paid ads without an upgrade. Delivered as-is with no color or audio guarantees.
10. **Deposit and retainer:** e.g., 50% to hold the date, nonrefundable and credited to the balance.
11. **Cancellation, postponement and kill fee:** a sliding scale tied to notice, plus a reschedule window.
12. **Payment:** due dates (Net 15, or balance due before final delivery for new or promoter clients), accepted methods and processing fees, sales tax line (Q7), late fee or interest. A stated rate overrides the 6% default but must respect Texas Chapter 303 ceilings.
13. **Expenses and travel:** mileage, parking, Austin travel and tour per diem.
14. **Releases:** who gets them. The client handles crowd-notice signage and talent releases for ads; the shooter makes no representation that patrons are released (Q5).
15. **Liability:** limit to fees paid; equipment-failure and illness substitution clause; force majeure.
16. **Insurance:** COI and additional-insured on request, with any extra cost billed (Q6).
17. **Archive:** retention period for files and footage.
18. **Governing law and venue:** Texas, Bexar County, with a small-claims option.
19. **Independent-contractor status** and a W-9 on file (Q8).
20. **Optional:** a prohibition on using deliverables to train AI models, and a requirement that edits not be altered or re-cut without consent.

### Gaps

## 5. Releases: model and property releases, venue signage, minors, and Texas right of publicity

### Takeaway
A model release is generally **not needed for editorial use**, such as news or the shooter's own publication covering the night. It **is needed for advertising or commercial use** of identifiable people, and self-promotion can count as commercial. Crowd "filming in progress" signage is **notice, not consent**. It is useful as a fallback, but signed releases are needed for featured people in ads. Texas protects living people through a **common-law misappropriation** tort: appropriation of a name or likeness for its value, excluding incidental or newsworthy use. A **post-mortem** statute (Property Code ch. 26) covers deceased individuals.

### Cited Findings
- **ASMP:**
  - Editorial and fine-art images usually don't need releases.
  - Commercial uses do; self-promotion on a homepage, in direct mail or in a portfolio carries risk.
  - Covers and a publication's promotional reuse can be "commercial."
  - News images can still draw false-light claims.
  - Licensees' later uses are outside the shooter's control.
  - Earning income from an image doesn't by itself make the use commercial.
  - Giving the subject something of value is favored.
  - Source: [ASMP model release Q&A](https://www.asmp.org/strictly-business-blog/model-releases-questions-answers/); [ASMP model release page](https://www.asmp.org/copyright-and-law/model-release/)
- **Quick test (A Photo Editor, 2017):**
  - Identifiable people in marketing for a brand, product or cause → release.
  - Unrecognizable people → usually not.
  - Identity can be revealed by tattoos or distinctive settings, not just faces.
  - A newspaper or magazine that isn't promoting a company typically doesn't need releases.
  - Source: [A Photo Editor / Wonderful Machine (Sept 2017)](https://www.aphotoeditor.com/2017/09/27/expert-advice-releases-and-permits/)
- **FAU's decision tree** (credits ASMP): if a person is recognizable, ask whether the use is an advertisement; if not, whether it is commercial (brochures, packaging, promotional websites). A yes to either means a release is needed. — [FAU release forms](https://www.fau.edu/university-communications/photography/release-forms)
- **ASMP Colorado** says releases are needed for advertising, marketing, packaging, stock or brand licensing, and **AI-altered images** using a person's likeness. Colorado-specific, but a useful checklist. — [ASMP Colorado](https://www.asmp.org/colorado/photography-model-release-colorado/)
- **Texas misappropriation elements** (*Matthews v. Wozencraft*, 15 F.3d 432, 437 (5th Cir. 1994)):
  - the defendant appropriated the plaintiff's name or likeness for its value, **not incidentally or for a newsworthy purpose**
  - the plaintiff is identifiable
  - the defendant gained some advantage or benefit
  - *Matthews* is a federal court's prediction of Texas law, so state courts are not bound by it.
  - Sources: [15 F.3d 432 (law.resource.org)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/015/15.F3d.432.93-4434.html); [Brown v. Ames (5th Cir. 2000)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/98/98-20736.CV0.wpd.pdf)
- In Texas, misappropriation and the common-law right of publicity are treated as synonyms. *Henley v. Dillard Department Stores* (N.D. Tex. 1999) found a violation where an ad traded on Don Henley's recognizable identity. — [Digital Media Law Project, Texas Right of Publicity](https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/texas-right-publicity-law)
- **Texas Property Code ch. 26** ("Buddy Holly" statute) creates a property right in a **deceased** individual's name, voice, signature, photograph or likeness for 50 years after death. For living people, the common-law tort is the practical route. — [Tex. Prop. Code ch. 26](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/PR/htm/PR.26.htm); [DMLP](https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/texas-right-publicity-law)
- **Texas Music Office resource:** the Governor's Texas Music Office hosts a page on misappropriation and right of publicity. Not retrieved; listed as a resource. — [Texas Music Office](https://gov.texas.gov/music/page/misappropriation_and_right_of_publicity)
- **Signage is notice, not consent:** a sign informs, while consent is a revocable choice, and a filming notice has no signature while a release depends on one. — [Fora Soft on consent and notice](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/video-surveillance/articles-vms/consent-and-notice-for-surveillance); [Business-in-a-Box filming notice template](https://www.business-in-a-box.com/template/filming-notice-D13973)
- **How organizers handle signage:**
  - Boston University accepts that signed releases from everyone are unworkable at big events and uses door signage when marketing use is planned. — [BU photo release signage](https://sites.bu.edu/marcom-team/departments/photography/photo-release-signage-events)
  - GitLab pairs likeness-release language in registration terms with on-site posters. — [GitLab handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/legal/publicity-waiver-release/)
  - A 2024 production notice letter required dated photos showing where notices were posted; footage without that proof needed individual talent releases. — [IFCJ 2024 film notice letter](https://www.ifcj.org/wp-content/uploads/blessing-israel-2024-film-notice-letter-en.pdf)
  - CalArts advises avoiding anyone who objects and using signed releases when in doubt. — [CalArts handbook](https://2pop.calarts.edu/student-handbook/likenesses/)
- **"Crowd release" notices are contested.** One law-firm blog reads them as broad agreements; informal commentary disputes that someone who never saw the sign agreed. — [Gonzalo Law](https://gonzalolaw.com/how-was-i-filmed-without-consent-the-crowd-release-notice/)
- **Texas bills:** a 2007 Texas bill (H.B. 1804) stated that signs alone don't establish consent under its provision. It appears tied to places with a reasonable expectation of privacy; whether it was enacted was not confirmed. — [Tex. H.B. 1804 (80R) text](https://www.legis.state.tx.us/tlodocs/80R/billtext/html/HB01804F.HTM)
- **Minors:** institutional guidance treats minors separately, with heightened consent rules. The only page retrieved is Canadian, not US law. — [SFU minors guidance](https://www.sfu.ca/students/photo-video-permissions/campus-outdoor-events/minors.html)

### Inferences
- **Free public nightlife galleries** in the shooter's own publication look like editorial or newsworthy coverage, which the Texas tort excludes. Risk rises when the same images are used to **sell**: ads for the publication, sponsor posts, or venue or promoter paid ads featuring identifiable patrons. **[Legal interpretation]**
- **Practical policy:**
  - Post "photography and video in progress" signage at the door and booth. Keep dated photos of the signage.
  - Honor takedown requests from patrons promptly.
  - Don't feature identifiable patrons in paid or ad deliverables without signed releases. A phone-based e-release on the night is workable.
  - Write into client licenses that ad use of crowd images requires the client to secure releases. **[Inference]**
- **Performers:**
  - Concert images used editorially are low-risk.
  - Artist-commissioned work is used with the artist's consent; the contract should say so.
  - Using a performer's likeness to promote a third-party brand or the shooter's own services is commercial and needs permission.
  - Photo-pass terms may restrict use further (see Q2 and the credentials researcher). **[Inference]**
- **Minors at all-ages DIY and house shows:** avoid featuring identifiable minors in galleries where possible, and get a **parent or guardian** signature for any commercial use of a minor's image. This is general practice, not verified for Texas in this research. **[Legal interpretation; verify]**
- **Property releases:** venue interiors, artwork and murals in ads can raise property or trademark issues. Not researched; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- No US or Texas-specific authority was retrieved on **minors' releases** (parent/guardian signature requirements, age of majority for releases).
- No source on **property releases** for venues, murals or logos in nightlife ads.
- Whether any Texas statute gives legal effect to "notice of filming" signage at public events was not confirmed; H.B. 1804's status is unknown.
- The Texas Music Office page and a recent Texas state-court misappropriation decision were not retrieved.

## 7. Texas business setup: sole proprietor + DBA vs. LLC, franchise tax, and sales and use tax

### Takeaway
- **Sole proprietor + DBA:** the Bexar County assumed-name filing costs about **$13–$21** and lasts 10 years.
- **LLC:** costs **$300** to form (Form 205). It must file an annual **Public Information Report** by May 15, but owes **no franchise tax** below the **$2.65M** threshold for 2026–2027 reports. The separate No Tax Due Report was eliminated starting with 2024 reports.
- **Sales tax** **[Tax interpretation]:** the Texas Comptroller treats photography and videography that deliver photos or files, **including digital delivery**, as taxable sales. That includes related session or sitting fees. The combined rate is up to **8.25%** in San Antonio and Austin.
  - A free **sales tax permit** is required.
  - Licensing rights without delivering a copy, and selling a "master recording," may be nontaxable.
  - Bundling a license with file delivery risks the whole charge being taxable. Confirm with the Comptroller.

### Cited Findings

**Sole proprietor + DBA (Bexar County)**
- **Fees** (Bexar County Clerk's published schedule): **$13** for one owner plus $0.50 per additional owner.
  - A county FAQ lists **$14**.
  - The application form shows **$20** total with a notary's acknowledgment, or **$21** if the clerk acknowledges.
  - A clerk's deputy can acknowledge for $1.
  - Copies cost $2 plain or $7 certified.
- **Process:**
  - The form must be notarized or acknowledged, and every co-owner signs.
  - Registrations **expire after 10 years**.
  - File in person at the Paul Elizondo Tower (101 W. Nueva, Suite 112) or other county locations, or by mail.
  - The county form is for **unincorporated** businesses; entities such as LLCs file with the Secretary of State.
  - Clerk phone: (210) 335-2223.
- Sources: [Bexar County Assumed Names/DBAs](https://www.bexar.org/2958); [Bexar instructions](https://www.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/20344/Instructions-for-Assumed-Name-Certificates); [Bexar application form](https://www.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/26459/Assumed-Name-Application-for-Certificate-of-Ownership); [Bexar FAQ](https://www.bexar.org/m/faq?cat=70)

**LLC formation**
- **Fee:** the Texas LLC Certificate of Formation (Form 205) costs **$300**, citing Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code §§ 4.152 and 4.154. Card payments carry about a 2.7% convenience fee.
- **Filing:** online via SOSDirect.
- **Requirements:** a registered agent (not the company itself) with a physical Texas street address; mail drops don't count.
- These are secondary sources (Zenind, dated July 29, 2026; Ezel); verify on sos.state.tx.us. — [Zenind Form 205 guide](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/texas-form-205-how-to-file-a-certificate-of-formation-for-an-llc); [Ezel Texas LLC formation](https://ezel.ai/surveys/domestic-llc-formation-filing-requirements/texas)

**Franchise tax**
- **No-tax-due threshold:** **$2.65 million** for reports due in **2026 and 2027**, up from $2.47M for 2024–2025.
- **Rates:** 0.375% for retail and wholesale, 0.75% for others. EZ computation of 0.331% for total revenue ≤ $20M. Compensation deduction cap $480,000.
- **No Tax Due Report eliminated** for reports originally due on or after Jan 1, 2024. Entities at or under the threshold must still file the **Public Information Report (PIR)** or Ownership Information Report.
- **Due date:** typically **May 15**.
- **Penalties:** missing reports can bring penalties and **forfeiture of the right to do business**.
- **Revenue definition:** guides describe it differently (gross vs. annualized total revenue); confirm the definition.
- Sources: [Texas Comptroller, Franchise Tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/) (via search extract); [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Texas-Publishes-Franchise-Tax--60265); [Beancount (May 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/17/texas-franchise-tax-public-information-report-pir-no-tax-due-2026-form-05-102-2-65-million-threshold-forfeiture-reinstatement-guide); [Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/guides/texas-franchise-tax/)

**Sales and use tax on photography and videography (Texas Comptroller)**
- **Taxable Services (Pub 96-259, dated January 2026)** lists **photography and videography** among labor that produces a taxable item. It points to **Rule 3.312** ("Graphic Arts or Related Occupations; Miscellaneous Activities"). — [Comptroller Pub 96-259, Taxable Services](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-259.php)
- **Tax Policy News (Oct 2021):**
  - Photos delivered as prints, **digital files or electronic files** are taxable.
  - **Sitting fees and reimbursed costs** that relate directly to producing and selling the photos are taxable when billed to the client.
  - Source: [Comptroller Tax Policy News, Oct 2021](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/tax-policy-news/2021-october.php)
- **Basis for taxing digital delivery:** Texas Tax Code § 151.009 defines tangible personal property to include things perceptible to the senses, which captures electronic image files. Taxability depends on where the buyer receives them. Lump-sum billing doesn't avoid tax. — [Imaging Spectrum (secondary)](https://imagingspectrum.com/blogs/blog/cause-im-the-texas-tax-man)
- **Pub 94-104 (Film, Video and Audio Production Companies):**
  - A **copy** of a master recording is taxable.
  - The producer's sale of the **master** and a **license** of rights (including broadcast rights) are not.
  - A video made for commercial distribution (e.g., a commercial or corporate film) can qualify as a master recording. **Wedding videos** fall outside the definition and, per the search extract, so do social-media clips. **Verify this detail.**
  - Converting videotapes to digital files is taxable data processing.
  - Source: [Comptroller Pub 94-104](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-104.php)
- **Pub 94-124 (Manufacturing Exemptions, Mar 2021):** production companies may buy certain items used directly to produce a film, video or sound master tax-free when the master is later sold, licensed or broadcast. Using exempt equipment for other purposes (e.g., personal photos) is taxable "divergent use." — [Comptroller Pub 94-124](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-124.php)
- **Mixed charges:** a combined charge can be split only if the nontaxable part is distinct and identifiable. An unseparated taxable part above **5%** of the contract price makes the whole charge taxable. This rule was written for services, and the search extract applies it to licenses by inference. — [Comptroller Pub 96-259 (via search extract)](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-259.php)
- **Pub 94-176 ("Photographers and Texas Sales Tax")** exists in the Comptroller's STAR index (2009 "Tax Topics" label), but its current status and text were not retrieved. — [STAR index entry](https://star.comptroller.texas.gov/subject-headings-for-document/200908894P)
- **Older Comptroller letters** (via Ezel summaries; old and fact-specific):
  - 1993: a glamour studio's session fee was taxable, tied to later photo sales. — [Ezel 9306L1243G03](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/9306l1243g03-glamour-photography-session-fees-wardrobe-makeover-private-photo)
  - 1995: a usage/licensing fee for publication rights was nontaxable; delivered photos were taxable absent a resale certificate. — [Ezel 9510527L](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/9510527l-copyrighted-photographs-images-illustrations-usage-fee-licensing-fee)
  - 1999: studios could buy cameras, flashes, scanners, image-editing computers and software, and set materials tax-free with an exemption certificate. This is an old, fact-specific ruling; whether it still applies, and to what use, needs confirming. — [Ezel 9911865L](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/9911865l-photographer-processor-equipment-tpp-used-in-producing-photographs)
  - **Conflict:** a 1991 letter treated motion-picture camera work as nontaxable while still photography was taxable. This conflicts with the 2026 Taxable Services publication listing videography. — [Ezel 9105L1115A12](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/9105l1115a12-still-photo-shoots-not-exempt)
  - A 2008 letter treating digital copies as taxable regardless of transfer method is marked **superseded as of March 4, 2020**. — [STAR 200812253L](https://star.comptroller.texas.gov/view/200812253L)
- **Comptroller policy on graphic design and photography:** Bloomberg Tax (Dec 2019) reported a Comptroller policy notice on this topic; full text is paywalled. — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-state/texas-comptroller-issues-sales-tax-policy-news-on-graphic-design-photography)
- **Permit:**
  - Anyone engaged in business in Texas who sells taxable items must hold a sales tax permit and collect and remit tax.
  - **The permit is free**, though a security bond may be required.
  - Source: [Comptroller permit FAQ](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/faq/permit.php); [Pub 96-259](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-259.php)
- **Rates:**
  - State 6.25%, plus up to 2% local, for a maximum combined **8.25%**. — [Comptroller Sales and Use Tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/)
  - **Austin** is listed at 8.25% (city 1% + transit 1%). Zamp shows some Austin ZIPs lower. — [Kintsugi Austin (Sept 2026)](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/texas/austin); [Quaderno Austin 2026](https://quaderno.io/guides/austin-sales-tax)
  - **San Antonio** is mostly 8.25%, but some ZIPs (e.g., 78243) are 6.75%, and the local split varies by source. — [Kintsugi San Antonio](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/texas/san-antonio); [TaxHero](https://taxhero.net/sales-tax/city/tx-san-antonio/); [Sales Tax Handbook](https://www.salestaxhandbook.com/texas/rates/san-antonio)
  - The rate is set by the delivery location; use the Comptroller's address-based rate lookup.

### Inferences
- **Entity choice.**
  - Sole proprietorship + Bexar DBA is the cheapest way to operate under a brand name.
  - An LLC costs $300 up front, plus a registered agent and the annual PIR, and owes **$0 franchise tax** at this revenue level.
  - The LLC's main benefit is separating business liability, which is more relevant given venue, crowd and touring exposure. Insurance does much of the practical risk work either way. **[Legal interpretation]**
  - If the shooter forms an LLC, its assumed name (e.g., the publication's name) is filed with the **Secretary of State**, not Bexar County. Fee not verified; see Gaps.
- **Sales tax is the most consequential compliance item.** **[Tax interpretation; confirm with the Comptroller or a CPA]** Under current Comptroller guidance:
  - **Event photo deliverables** (digital galleries delivered to a client) and their **session or coverage fees** are likely **taxable** at the local rate.
  - **Event recap videos, verticals and social reels** delivered to Texas clients look taxable as "videography" producing a taxable item. They probably don't qualify as nontaxable "master recordings" (social clips and wedding-type videos are reportedly excluded).
  - **Music videos and brand films** made for commercial distribution may fit the "master recording" concept, under which sale of the master or a rights license is nontaxable but copies are taxable. This is fact-specific.
  - **Editing or color hourly work on a client's own footage** is unclear: it could be labor producing a taxable item, or data processing. Ask the Comptroller.
  - **Separately stated licensing or usage fees** may be nontaxable, but only if distinct and identifiable on the invoice, and bundling can taint the whole charge.
  - **Prints** sold from the publication's galleries are taxable sales.
  - Practical steps:
    - Get the free permit.
    - Add "+ applicable sales tax" to the rate card.
    - Itemize coverage or production, deliverables and licensing on invoices.
    - Collect **resale certificates** from agencies that resell the work.
    - Consider a written request to the Comptroller's Tax Policy Division for the video and editing questions.
- **Purchases.** Production-company manufacturing exemptions (Pub 94-124; 1999 letter) could make some gear purchases tax-exempt if used to produce items for sale or masters. Misuse creates taxable divergent use, and eligibility should be confirmed before claiming. **[Tax interpretation]**

### Takeaway
Client-management tools for solo photographers cost roughly:
- HoneyBook: $29–$129/month
- Dubsado: $35–$55/month after its December 2025 price reset
- Studio Ninja: about $24–$53/month
- Wave: free invoicing, Pro $19/month

Card processing runs about 2.9% + a per-transaction fee.

- **Quarterly estimates** are due Apr 15, Jun 15 and Sep 15, 2026 and Jan 15, 2027.

### Inferences
- **Terms by client type** (best-practice synthesis, not sourced as an industry norm):
  - **New promoters and one-off events:** 50% nonrefundable deposit to book, with the balance due **before final delivery**. Send watermarked or low-res previews (or one teaser vertical) until paid. Full-res files and the license activate on payment. Card or ACH via the invoicing tool.
  - **Established venues and brands:** 50% deposit, then **Net 15** with a stated late fee (e.g., a flat fee plus monthly interest). The contract-stated rate must stay within Texas Chapter 303 limits. Automated reminders (HoneyBook Essentials SMS reminders, Dubsado Premier automations) reduce chasing.
  - **Retainers:** bill monthly in advance, auto-charged.
  - **Collections path:**
    1. Reminder sequence.
    2. Suspension of the content license (no usage until paid; the license should be conditioned on payment).
    3. Demand letter.
    4. Bexar County justice court (≤$20k).
  - **[Legal interpretation for license-suspension and late-fee mechanics]**
- **Processing fees:** at about 2.9% + 25–30¢, card fees on a $2,200 retainer are about $64–$65. Encourage ACH or bank transfer, or price fees in. **[Inference]**
- **1099s:** fewer forms will arrive in 2026, since clients only issue a 1099-NEC at $2,000+. The shooter must still report all income, including small promoter payments and Venmo or Cash App business receipts below the 1099-K threshold.
- **Reserves:** setting aside **about 25–30%** of net profit for federal income tax plus SE tax (Texas has no state income tax) and paying quarterly is a common rule of thumb. That range is not sourced here, and the SE tax alone is about 14.1% of net profit. **[Tax interpretation; confirm with a CPA]**
- **Sales tax collected** (Q7) is not income. Keep it in a separate account and remit on the Comptroller's assigned filing schedule. **[Tax interpretation]**

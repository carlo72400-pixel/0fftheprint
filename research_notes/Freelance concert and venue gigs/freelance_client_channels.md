## 1. Which channels produce the most paid photo and video work? (surveys, lead-source and conversion data)

### Takeaway
Every photographer survey found puts referrals and repeat clients first. In Zenfolio's 2026 State of the Photography Industry survey, 85% of photographers rely on referrals and 95% rate them effective. Instagram is a distant second for being discovered, ahead of Facebook and Google. No 2024–2026 survey of videographers' lead sources, and no US-only or Texas-specific breakdown, was found. The little conversion data on pay-per-lead platforms shows about a 1-in-10 win rate, which means paying roughly $130–$1,300 per booked job.

### Cited Findings
**Surveys (photography; mostly global samples)**
- **Zenfolio SOPI 2026 (April 2026 release, also on ShootProof):** 85% of photographers use referrals to win new clients, and 95% rate referrals as effective. Instagram ranks second for perceived importance in being discovered, ahead of Facebook and Google. — [Zenfolio SOPI 2026](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2026-april); [ShootProof SOPI 2026](https://www.shootproof.com/sopi-2026-april); [Zenfolio 2026 survey results post](https://zenfolio.com/blog/2026-photography-industry-survey-results/). The 2026 sample size and country mix were not visible. The survey is global, not US-only.
- **Survey owner and earlier sample:** Zenfolio describes itself as the owner of the SOPI survey. An earlier edition (2024 or 2025; the excerpt was unclear) drew more than 4,500 photographers from more than 70 countries. — [Zenfolio SOPI 2025 page](https://zenfolio.com/blog/state-of-the-photography-industry-2025/)
- **SOPI 2025:** client acquisition was photographers' top challenge for the fourth year running, and 46.4% called it their main obstacle. — reported secondhand by [Trovatten, "How to Get Photography Clients in 2026"](https://trovatten.com/blog/get-photography-clients)
- **Zenfolio–Format study:** credits 61% of photography work to referrals and word of mouth. The citing blog dates the study to 2024. — reported secondhand by [Trovatten](https://trovatten.com/blog/get-photography-clients); the original was not located.
- **More 2025 figures from secondary summaries:**
  - 63% named "finding new clients" as a leading challenge.
  - 44% cited social-media algorithms as a challenge.
  - 56% wanted more help with social media (year unclear).
  - These surfaced in search excerpts of [Trovatten](https://trovatten.com/blog/get-photography-clients) and [Proud Marketer, networking guide (2025)](https://proudmarketer.com/networking-for-photographers/). Which page states which figure was unclear, so treat them as unverified.
- **aMi 2025 photographer survey:** "word of mouth/referrals" was the most-cited client source, at 66%. Instagram and SEO-focused homepages carried some weight. — [aMi Photographer Survey 2025](https://note.com/ami_inc/n/n08a261ac88a3?hl=en). Caution: aMi is a Japanese company publishing on note.com, so the sample is probably Japanese photographers, not US.
- **Aftershoot 2024 trends report:** reportedly ranked referrals and social media as the leading sources of work, with search optimization second. — surfaced via [Aftershoot blog (2026)](https://aftershoot.com/blog/social-media-for-photographers/); the original report was not seen.
- **Unsourced claims from marketing blogs:**
  - Proud Marketer (2025) says over 70% of clients found their photographer on Instagram or TikTok, and that photographers who post consistently get twice the inquiries of referral-only photographers. — [Proud Marketer social guide](https://proudmarketer.com/social-media-for-photographers/)
  - SchedulingKit (2026) says 67% of clients use Google to find photographers. — [SchedulingKit statistics page](https://www.schedulingkit.com/statistics/photography-business-statistics)
  - Neither page cites a source. Do not present these as data.
- **Separate commercial/editorial survey:** the State of the Photo Industry 2026 survey (A Photo Editor / Wonderful Machine) collected responses until June 8, 2026, including questions on promotional strategies. No results were found. — [Wonderful Machine call for responses](https://wonderfulmachine.com/article/call-to-action-for-the-state-of-the-photo-industry-2026/)

**Conversion evidence from pay-per-lead platforms**
- **Thumbtack, headshot photographer:** paid about $50 per lead on $150 sessions and won about 1 in 10. That is about $500 of lead spend for each $150 job. — [Thumbtack Community thread](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/7733) (undated; this forum's threads carry comments from 2021 to 2025)
- **LeadCapture (2023-era):**
  - Photographers pay about $20–$75 per Thumbtack lead.
  - Anecdotal close rates run 10–30%.
  - A hypothetical $20 lead at a 15% close rate works out to about $133 per new customer.
  - — [LeadCapture: Thumbtack lead cost](https://leadcapture.io/blog/thumbtack-lead-cost/)
- **Bark (UK cost guide, updated Aug 30, 2026):**
  - Photographers who reply quickly book about a third of the leads they reach; slow repliers book about a sixth.
  - Users report that more than half of paid leads never respond, and credits spent on them are not refunded.
  - At 12 credits per lead and 1 booking in 10, the cost per booking is about £260.
  - — [Whito: Bark cost UK](https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/)
- **DPReview forum (undated):** counting the leads you pay for but don't win roughly doubles the real cost per lead. — [DPReview thread "Has anyone used Bark.com?"](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4473162)
- **Snappr off-platform anecdote:** a Melbourne Snappr photographer said that handing a client their contact details led to well-paid video work off the platform. Doing that breaks Snappr's non-circumvention policy. — [Inside Imaging, 2019 (US photographer's experience)](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/snappr-experience-low-pay-and-unrealistic-expectations/); [Inside Imaging, 2019 ("Life as a Snappr photographer")](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/life-as-a-snappr-photographer/). The excerpt didn't make clear which of these two articles contains the Melbourne anecdote.

## 2. Marketplaces and lead platforms: fees, lead costs, job quality, sentiment (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Consumer lead marketplaces charge per lead whether or not you are hired. Thumbtack sells leads at a price the pro sets, and photography leads are reported at about $50–$170. Bark sells credits. Forum sentiment through mid-2025 is mostly negative about cost against bookings. GigSalad's model is the lowest-risk: free with a 5% fee on booked jobs, or paid tiers with 2.5%. Snappr data is old (2019–2020) and reports low pay and a punitive non-circumvention clause. Upwork moved to a variable 0–15% fee in May 2025. Wedio turned out to be a gear-rental marketplace, not a videographer-hiring network.

### Cited Findings
**Thumbtack**
- **How pricing works:**
  - Pros set a price per lead, a weekly spending limit and a prepaid balance.
  - Lead cost varies with category, job size, number of competing pros and local market.
  - When the weekly budget runs out, leads stop until the Monday reset.
  - On some jobs Thumbtack takes a share of what the customer pays instead of charging up front.
  - "Opportunities" (outreach the pro starts) are billed only if the customer replies.
  - — [Thumbtack help: first two weeks guide](https://help.thumbtack.com/article/new-pro-guide-1) (undated)
- **Prices pros report:**
  - About $60–$80 for wedding leads and $70–$130 per photography inquiry.
  - $50–$170 per lead in general.
  - One pro paid about $6 per lead at first and now sees $30 or more.
  - One pro paid over $150 when they were the only pro chosen, against about $50 when other pros were also chosen.
  - — [Thumbtack Community: Cost of leads as a photographer](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/1057/cost-of-leads-as-a-photographer-and-refunds); [Lead Prices](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/218/lead-prices); [Expensive leads for cheap jobs](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/1039/expensive-leads-for-cheap-jobs) (comments dated Nov 2021 to Jun 2025)
- **Charges without bookings:** pros say they are charged even when the customer never books, and several pros can pay for the same lead. — [Thumbtack Community: expensive leads but no bookings](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/1818/anyone-else-struggling-with-expensive-leads-but-no-bookings)
- **General range:** lead costs run from about $10 to over $100. Thumbtack does not publish its lead prices. — [Housecall Pro explainer](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/)

**Bark**
- **Credit pricing (UK guide, updated Aug 30, 2026):**
  - List price is £1.80 plus VAT per credit; discounted packs run about £1.20–£1.50.
  - Pack prices are visible only after you create a pro account.
  - In the guide's photography example, a lead costs 12 credits, so 10 leads cost about £216 before VAT.
  - The guide says job value should be 5–7 times the lead price: a £1,200 wedding can justify the spend, but a £150 portrait session probably cannot.
  - Photographers get lower response rates than tradespeople, and buyers shop around on price.
  - — [Whito Bark cost guide](https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/)
- **Lead sizes:** typical leads cost 5–20 credits (about £7–£40), and wedding photography is one of the premium-priced categories. — [Sharetribe, citing NobleLeads](https://www.sharetribe.com/create/how-to-build-website-like-bark.com.md)
- **US data point (undated):** $24 to contact a home-photography lead that could go to as many as five photographers. — [DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4473162)
- **UK forum:** one photographer tried 10 leads and won no work. — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/experience-with-bark-com.373033/post-2843063)

**GigSalad** (event-entertainment marketplace with photographer and videographer categories)
- **Membership tiers:**
  - **Free:** $0, 5% booking fee, 2 categories, deposits up to $500, 10 photos, client phone numbers hidden until a booking is made.
  - **Pro:** $139 a quarter or $359 a year, 2.5% booking fee, 15 categories, deposits up to $1,000, 50 photos. GigSalad claims Pro members get 16 times more leads than Free members.
  - **Featured:** $169 a quarter or $479 a year, 2.5% booking fee, 20 categories, deposits up to $2,000, 100 photos. GigSalad claims about 26 times the leads of Free.
  - — [GigSalad membership pricing](https://help.gigsalad.com/article/148-membership-pricing) (undated). The [Sidehusl review](https://sidehusl.com/gigsalad/) dates its plan details to 2023.
- **How the booking fee works:**
  - It is charged on the full job price and withheld from both the deposit and the balance.
  - Receiving leads and sending quotes is free.
  - Clients pay their own separate service fee of about 10–12%.
  - If a client pays you directly, the job must still be marked as booked, and the fee still applies.
  - — [GigSalad provider service fees](https://help.gigsalad.com/article/97-vendor-service-fees); [Booking requirement for free members](https://help.gigsalad.com/article/142-booking-requirement-free-members)

**The Bash** (formerly GigMasters)
- **Pricing terms:**
  - Membership prices are not published; the pricing page tells vendors to call 866-342-9794.
  - New sign-ups and upgrades get a 30-day refund window; renewals do not.
  - Plans renew automatically for the same term unless cancelled within 30 days before the term ends.
  - Promotional prices sometimes undercut the usual rate.
  - Bookings paid through the site can carry a service fee set as a percentage of the vendor's price.
  - — [The Bash pricing & payments](https://thebash.com/pricing-payments) (undated)
- **Vendor price examples:** listings show starting prices such as a Maryland videographer from $50 an hour and a California photographer from $800 per event. — [The Bash videographer listing](https://www.thebash.com/videographer/stampdmv); [The Bash photographer listing](https://www.thebash.com/photographer/jojo)

**Snappr** (all data 2019–2020; nothing newer was found)
- **Platform cut:** Snappr keeps 20–35% of each booking. — [Inside Imaging, 2019](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/snappr-experience-low-pay-and-unrealistic-expectations/)
- **Client packages:** prices are fixed no matter which photographer takes the job. They ran from about US$45 (AU$59) for 30 minutes and 5 images to about US$340 for 7 hours and 30 images. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=1031454). Package details conflict with [Snappr's blog](https://www.snappr.com/blog/where-snappr-fits-in), which says the AU$59 package includes 3 photos.
- **A US Snappr photographer (2019) reported:**
  - a steady run of 30- and 60-minute jobs paying under US$100;
  - good reviews did not lead to more assignments;
  - a US$5,000 penalty for sharing contact details with customers.
  - — [Inside Imaging, 2019](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/snappr-experience-low-pay-and-unrealistic-expectations/)
- **Funding:** Snappr raised $13M in 2020 and was billed as the "Uber of photography." — [PetaPixel, Oct 6, 2020](https://petapixel.com/2020/10/06/snappr-has-raised-13m-to-be-the-uber-of-photography/)
- **Pay estimate:** Indeed shows about $89 an hour, based on a single reported salary. — [Indeed: Snappr salaries](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Snappr/salaries)

**Upwork**
- **Fee since May 1, 2025:** the freelancer service fee varies from 0% to 15% per contract. The rate is shown at the proposal or offer stage and stays fixed for that contract. Reported drivers include demand for the skill, market saturation, project complexity and track record. — [Jobbers.io (a competing platform)](https://www.jobbers.io/why-upwork-takes-20-of-your-money-and-how-to-keep-100/); [GigRadar, 2026](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-alternatives-in-2026-free-or-less-expensive-options-for-freelancers)
- **Effective rate and other costs:**
  - The typical effective rate is about 10% according to Jobbers, or about 12–13% according to GigRadar.
  - Connects cost about 15 cents each.
  - Fees on direct contracts are reported inconsistently.
  - The variable model was still in place as of July 2026.
  - — [Jobbers fee encyclopedia](https://www.jobbers.io/the-freelance-platform-fee-encyclopedia-every-fee-on-every-platform-documented/); [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-alternatives-in-2026-free-or-less-expensive-options-for-freelancers)

**Video-specific networks and crew-booking firms**
- **Wedio:**
  - A Danish peer-to-peer rental marketplace for cameras, lenses and drones, rented by the day, week or month with global insurance.
  - A directory profile puts its commission at about 17% per rental.
  - It raised €1.25M in April 2023.
  - No evidence was found that it runs a marketplace for hiring videographers.
  - — [EU-Startups directory](https://www.eu-startups.com/directory/wedio-2/); [Tech.eu, Apr 18, 2023](https://tech.eu/2023/04/18/danish-camera-sharing-platform-wedio-raises-eur125-million)
- **Shoots.video:** a client-facing directory and marketplace of freelance cinematographers and camera crews. It has San Antonio and Austin pages and a job board. One posting, for a San Jose corporate event in April 2025, paid $900 flat for two days. — [Shoots.video US camera crews](https://www.shoots.video/production/us/camera-crew-support/); [San Antonio photographers page](https://www.shoots.video/production/san-antonio/photographer); [Event camera operator job](https://www.shoots.video/job/event-camera-operator/)
- **Twine:** claims more than 14,500 camera operators listed, but at least one profile tagged as a camera operator describes a marketing background. — [Twine camera operators](https://www.twine.net/find/camera-operators/us/mckinney)
- **Beverly Boy Productions:** a national crew-booking company. Its San Antonio careers page recruits freelance camera operators who bring their own equipment. — [Beverly Boy San Antonio video production jobs](https://beverlyboy.com/careers/video-production-jobs-in-san-antonio/). Caution: its pages are templated SEO copy with errors, for example a "San Antonio, Paraguay" page. — [example page](https://beverlyboy.com/san-antonio-paraguay-videographer-services-3/)
- **Network news field producers:** Fox News Media posts as-needed freelance field producer roles in New York, Chicago and Miami (2025–2026). No Texas posting was found. — [Fox Careers: Miami freelance field producer, Jan 30, 2025](https://foxcareers.com/Search/JobDetail/R50024207/freelance-field-producer-miami-bureau-fox-news-media)

### Inferences
- **Lead-cost arithmetic for personal events (quince, birthday):** at about $50–$130 per photography lead and about a 10% win rate, each booked job costs roughly $500–$1,300 in leads. Bark's rule of thumb (job value 5–7 times the lead price) points the same way. These platforms only pay off for packages in the low four figures or higher.
- **GigSalad:** the Free tier is the lowest-risk test, because the 5% fee applies only to booked jobs. The Pro tier ($359 a year) pays for itself on fee savings alone once yearly GigSalad bookings pass about $14,360 (2.5% of $14,360 is $359), not counting any extra leads.
- **Snappr:** the 2019 terms (20–35% cut, sub-$100 jobs, $5,000 non-circumvention penalty) cut against this shooter's model, which is building direct repeat relationships.
- **Speed of reply:** the Bark data (about a third booked by fast repliers against a sixth by slow ones) suggests that on any marketplace, fast replies matter as much as lead price.
- **Upwork:** may suit remote Resolve grading or editing work more than local shoots. The fee is now set per contract, so check it before bidding.

### Gaps
- **Fiverr:** the seller commission was not verified (search budget ran out).
- **Upwork's own fee page:** blocked, so the fees above are secondhand. Jobbers.io says the pre-May-2025 structure was a tiered 20/10/5%. That may describe the schedule from before 2023 and was not verified, so do not repeat it without checking.
- **Missing prices:** Bark's US credit prices, The Bash's membership prices and Thumbtack's official lead prices for San Antonio photography and videography were not found.
- **Snappr:** no 2024–2026 data on its pay or terms was found.
- **Not researched (search budget):** freelance video networks such as Crews Control, Storyhunter, Lemonlight and Stringr. No "field-producer network" with a Texas presence was identified.
- **Missing sentiment:** no 2024–2026 Reddit threads specific to these platforms were retrieved.

## 3. Crew and production job boards and networks (ProductionHUB, Staff Me Up, Mandy, Backstage, LinkedIn, Facebook/Discord groups)

### Takeaway
Crew boards are cheap, from free to $139 a month. They surface commercial, corporate, TV and live-event day-rate calls, with posted camera-operator examples at $400–$800 a day, alongside a lot of low-paid or unpaid indie work. In Texas, the main free crew-call channels are the Texas Film Commission Job Hotline and statewide or Austin Facebook groups such as Central Texas Film Makers and Texas Crew and Casting Calls. No San Antonio–specific paid-crew Facebook group and no Austin or San Antonio Discord server surfaced.

### Cited Findings
**ProductionHUB**
- **Profile tiers:**
  - **Basic:** $6.99 a month after a 30-day free period, with limited access to RFPs and project leads and 1 free response credit a month.
  - **Featured:** from $69 a month.
  - **Featured+:** from $139 a month.
  - Featured and Featured+ get unlimited local leads and response credits, plus homepage and top-of-search placement. Featured+ adds targeted banners.
  - — [ProductionHUB profile plans](https://www.productionhub.com/profiles/matrix) (undated)
- **Employer side:** job posts cost from US$75 each, with bulk discounts of up to 25%. Clients can submit crew and vendor requests for free. — [Betterteam on ProductionHUB](https://betterteam.com/ca/productionhub)
- **Credits:** freelance crew answer opportunities with credits, bought in packs or one at a time. — [SVG article on ProductionHUB features](https://www.sportsvideo.org/?p=63063) (undated)

**Staff Me Up**
- **Plans for job seekers:** Basic is free but capped at 5 applications. Premium costs $19.99 a month, or $149.99 a year (about $12.50 a month). Perks include job alerts, application promotion, analytics and résumé featuring. — [Staff Me Up support: Which subscription plan is right for me?](https://support.staffmeup.com/en/articles/11721473) (undated)
- **Posted camera-operator day rates (years not visible):**
  - $400 for an 8-hour day (live special event, San Simeon, CA). — [posting](https://staffmeup.com/jobs/Camera-Operator-San-Simeon-CA-Live-Special-Event-701679/apply)
  - $700 a day (TV, New York). — [posting](https://staffmeup.com/jobs/Camera-Operator-New-York-NY-TV-747318/apply)
  - $575–$800 a day, with extra fees possible for operators whose kit suits the production (Albuquerque). — [posting](https://staffmeup.com/jobs/Camera-Operator-Albuquerque-NM-Digital-755850/apply)
- **Austin example:** a one-day local camera operator for a web series presented by Sports Illustrated (Racing Quest), shooting at Circuit of the Americas. The operator brings their own camera, gimbal, sticks and lights and records interview sound. No pay was listed. — [Staff Me Up: Racing Quest at COTA](https://staffmeup.com/jobs/Camera-Operator-Austin-TX-Digital-672565/apply)

**Mandy**
- **Price:** premium costs about $7.95 a month or $50 a year according to [Sidehusl's Mandy review](https://sidehusl.com/mandy/) (undated). A competing directory estimates $15–$20 a month. — [ProductionList vs Mandy](https://www.productionlist.com/compare/mandy)
- **Sources conflict on applying:** Sidehusl says applying requires a paid membership. ProductionList says creating a profile and responding is free.
- **Listing quality:** per Sidehusl, the "jobs" section has a local-minimum-wage floor, while "opportunities" may be unpaid. Many listings are non-union or student projects paying well under scale. — [Sidehusl](https://sidehusl.com/mandy/)

**Texas Film Commission Job Hotline** (free)
- **How it works:**
  - A free public board of current and upcoming Texas productions: features, TV, commercials, animation and games.
  - It is updated each day new or revised postings come in.
  - Crew calls sit under "TV & Film Jobs," each with a contact for sending résumés.
  - Unpaid gigs are excluded unless they are verified internships.
  - Productions may not charge applicants.
  - The commission recommends getting pay terms in writing.
  - — [TFC Job Hotline](https://gov.texas.gov/film/hotline); [TV & Film Jobs](https://gov.texas.gov/film/hotline/crew); [Job Hotline rules](https://gov.texas.gov/film/page/job_hotline_rules_requirements)

## 4. Texas directories: how to get listed, cost, fit (TFC Texas Production Directory, FilmSA, Austin, Texas Music Office, AFS, City of San Antonio)

### Takeaway
All of the state and city directories found are free:
- **Texas Production Directory (Texas Film Commission):** needs a current Texas address and at least one documented paid professional credit for each category you list in. Relevant categories are Camera Operator, Unit Still Photographer and Product Photographer.
- **Austin Production Directory (Austin Film Commission):** for Austin-based crew with film, TV or commercial credits.
- **Texas Music Industry Directory (Texas Music Office):** free, with "Photographers" and "Video production" subcategories. This is the closest fit for a music photo and video shooter.

The San Antonio Film Commission runs a crew and vendor Production Directory, but how to register could not be verified. No City of San Antonio creative directory was found.

### Cited Findings
**Texas Production Directory (TPD), Texas Film Commission**
- **Eligibility:**
  - Listing is free and covers film, TV, commercials, animation, VFX, video games and XR.
  - You need a current Texas address.
  - Each category you select needs at least one documented, paid professional credit.
  - Listings that miss a requirement or lack supporting documents are rejected.
  - Some categories (associations, festivals, film commissions, school graduates, health-and-safety officers) carry extra requirements.
  - — [TPD rules & requirements](https://gov.texas.gov/film/page/tpd_rules_requirements)
- **Listing limits and upkeep:**
  - Up to 8 categories, with up to 7 credits each.

  - A résumé and links to a website, IMDb page or portfolio are strongly recommended, and a headshot helps.
  - The online form takes about 10–20 minutes and times out if left idle.
  - Use "Login / Create Listing" under the Directory menu.
  - — [TPD rules & requirements](https://gov.texas.gov/film/page/tpd_rules_requirements); [TPD overview](https://gov.texas.gov/film/page/texas-production-directory)

## 5. Live-event camera work: IMAG and camera calls at arenas and stadiums; IATSE Local 76 (San Antonio) and Local 205 (Austin)

### Takeaway
The documented way into arena and theater show calls is the IATSE overhire or referral list:
- **Local 76 (San Antonio):** send a short résumé to the secretary. Calls are dispatched through the CallSteward app.
- **Local 205 (Austin):** runs a referral hall with A, B, C and Extra lists, a $50 fee for non-member ("permit") workers, a 5% assessment on earnings, and (per a 2022 notice) a June 1–Aug 15 registration window.

Neither local publishes a separate camera or IMAG list. Broadcast camera crews in Texas fall under IATSE Local 796. In-house venue video jobs are part-time and hourly, such as the Moody Center AV technician at $21 an hour in 2026. No Texas IMAG day rate was found. The closest US anecdote is $525–$575 a day for labor only in the Dallas market.

- **The pitch:** a short live or multicam reel (Alamodome concert, nightlife), 24–48-hour turnaround and Resolve grading. Send it to San Antonio live-event and AV producers that list IMAG (Lerner Productions) and to broadcast vendors serving Austin sports.

## 7. Social and search: Instagram, TikTok, Google Business Profile, keyword pages, followers to clients

### Takeaway
The only survey-grade signal found is that photographers rank Instagram second for discovery, after referrals and ahead of Facebook and Google (SOPI 2026). No 2024–2026 data was retrieved on how TikTok or Google Business Profile convert for photo and video services. The widely repeated statistics ("70% find via IG/TikTok," "67% use Google") have no stated source.

### Cited Findings
- **SOPI 2026:** Instagram is second only to referrals in perceived importance for being discovered, ahead of Facebook and Google. — [Zenfolio SOPI 2026](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2026-april); [ShootProof SOPI 2026](https://www.shootproof.com/sopi-2026-april)
- **Secondary summaries (attribution unclear):** 44% of photographers cite social-media algorithms as a challenge (2025), and 56% want more help with social media. — [Trovatten](https://trovatten.com/blog/get-photography-clients); [Proud Marketer](https://proudmarketer.com/networking-for-photographers/)

  - 67% of clients use Google to find photographers. — [SchedulingKit](https://www.schedulingkit.com/statistics/photography-business-statistics)
- **2026 posting-cadence guides** exist from Aftershoot and Format, but their contents were not retrieved. — [Aftershoot: Social media for photographers (2026)](https://aftershoot.com/blog/social-media-for-photographers/); [Format: weekly workflow for posts, Stories and Reels (2026)](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/social-media-planning-for-photographers)

## 8. Cold outreach and networking: practices, response rates, San Antonio and Austin communities

### Takeaway
No 2024–2026 response-rate benchmark or outreach template specific to creatives was retrieved. The evidence that exists points to speed and direct personal contact: fast responders book about twice as often on Bark, and union and crew entry runs through business agents and stewards, where existing connections help. Austin has several active film networking hubs (Austin Film Meet, Central Texas Film Makers, Austin Film Society/Austin Studios). San Antonio's options that surfaced are thinner: the Filmmakers of San Antonio collective, FilmSA programs and events such as the San Antonio Film Slam, and the San Antonio Film Festival.

### Cited Findings
- **Speed of reply:** on Bark, photographers who reply quickly book about a third of the leads they contact, against about a sixth for slow repliers. — [Whito Bark guide (Aug 2026)](https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/)
- **Union outreach:** for overhire, phone the local's business agent. A cold approach is hard without a connection; stewards book overhires; take nearly every call offered. — [ControlBooth thread](https://www.controlbooth.com/goto/post?id=171000) (dated forum advice)
- **How overhire calls go out:** some overhire lists run on email blasts that name the event, timing, venue and positions, and workers reply to accept. — [Emerson College overhire listing (Boston example)](https://talentally.com/job/overhire-stage-hand-240753)
- **Job Hotline applications:** Texas Film Commission crew postings list a contact for emailing résumés, and the commission advises getting pay terms in writing before work starts. — [TFC Job Hotline rules](https://gov.texas.gov/film/page/job_hotline_rules_requirements)
- **Austin networking:**
  - Austin Film Meet: mixers, workshops and production programs. — [Austin Film Meet FAQ](https://www.austinfilmmeet.com/faq-2/)
  - Austin Filmmakers Facebook group: discussion and finding collaborators. — [ATX Theatre film resources](https://www.atxtheatre.org/film)
  - Media ATX keeps a list of film and TV groups. — [Media ATX film groups](https://www.mediaatx.org/groups-film)
  - Austin Film Society runs Austin Studios production services. — [AFS Production Services](https://www.austinfilm.org/production-services/)
- **San Antonio networking:**
  - Filmmakers of San Antonio: weekly collaborative productions, open to new members. — [Filmmakers of San Antonio](http://filmmakersofsanantonio.weebly.com/)
  - San Antonio Film Slam, listed on the Texas Film Commission events calendar (date not visible). — [TFC event: San Antonio Film Slam](https://gov.texas.gov/film/event/san_antonio_film_slam)
  - San Antonio Film Festival. — [Wikipedia: San Antonio Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Film_Festival)
  - San Antonio made a 2026 "best cities for filmmakers" list. — [AOL, 2026](https://www.aol.com/news/san-antonio-lands-role-2026-210003981.html)
- **Statewide:** the Texas Film Commission event calendar has listed regional filmmaker mixers (example from 2016), and it publishes a monthly newsletter. — [TFC event: North Texas filmmaker mixer, Oct 2016](https://gov.texas.gov/film/event/north_texas_filmmaker_mixer_october_2016); [TFC August 2026 newsletter](https://gov.texas.gov/film/post/the-texas-film-commission-august-newsletter-2026)

### Inferences
- For a shooter who also runs a publication, coverage itself is the warm opener. Covering a venue's or promoter's show before pitching paid work turns a cold email into a follow-up. This is a strategic inference; no response-rate data supports or tests it.
- Personal contact with business agents, stewards and production managers appears to work better than applications alone in the union and crew world. The documented advice is to call, show up and take calls.
- Austin offers more film networking than San Antonio, which argues for regular Austin trips (Austin Film Meet mixers, AFS events) on top of San Antonio's collective and festival scene.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 cold-email or DM response-rate benchmarks were retrieved for creative services or in general.
- No outreach templates or follow-up cadence guidance were retrieved.

  - CreativeMornings chapters in San Antonio or Austin;

  - ASMP or PPA-affiliated Texas groups, such as the Texas Professional Photographers Association;

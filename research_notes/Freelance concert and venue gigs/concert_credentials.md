Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result excerpts and summaries of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. ## 1. Who approves a photo pass, how a request flows, lead times, what a request needs, and why requests get denied

### Takeaway
At touring shows, the artist's side approves the pass: the tour or artist publicist, sometimes management or the tour manager. The venue and the promoter (Live Nation, AEG, C3) mostly collect requests and hand out passes. Approval depends mainly on whether a real outlet will publish the coverage. At arenas, decisions often arrive in the last week or on show day. Pit allotments are shrinking: in 2026 some major tours admit no outside photographers at all.

### Cited Findings
**Who decides and how requests flow**
- Approval usually sits with the artist's publicist or a promoter, and depends mostly on whether a real outlet will publish the photos. To find the contact, check the artist's website, then search for the publicist or manager. The venue's press contact is the fallback. (July 20, 2025) — [DIY Photography](https://www.diyphotography.net/how-to-get-a-concert-photo-pass-breaking-into-live-music-photography/)
- The artist's publicist or manager is the usual contact. Promoters often route passes to the artist's team because that is written into their contracts. Going through the promoter adds steps, and the artist side still has to sign off. (undated) — [BookingAgentInfo blog](https://blog.bookingagentinfo.com/how-to-get-a-media-or-press-pass-for-events-and-concerts/)
- Other places to look for the contact: the artist site's contact page, the About section of the artist's Facebook page, a search for "[artist] publicist/manager", big PR firms' rosters, and the record label. A phone call sometimes gets further than an email. (undated) — [Stephanie Sheronovich](https://www.stephaniesheronovich.com/post/how-to-get-a-photo-pass-without-a-publication-yes-it-s-possible); [500px](https://iso.500px.com/how-to-get-a-photo-pass-for-concerts/)
- Scale of the gatekeeper's job: one publicist may handle 50 to 100 tour dates, each with dozens of photographer requests to approve or deny. (undated; iShootShows guide cluster, exact page not pinned down because pages could not be opened) — [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-photo-passes-for-concert-photography/)
- A video credential at one venue: its help page says the artist and tour must approve the request because the venue itself cannot (venue identity not verified). — [help.thecircuit.com](https://help.thecircuit.com/hc/en-us/articles/39592053448980-How-do-I-get-a-press-media-credential)

**San Antonio and Austin venue specifics**
- **Boeing Center at Tech Port** (formerly Tech Port Center + Arena, San Antonio) publishes a media request form. Its rules: credentials are often not settled until the last week or show day. Final say rests with tour management and publicists, not the venue. One request per outlet per event. Extra guest seats are not guaranteed. Critic seats go to outlets planning a written review. Independent photographers without an affiliated publication are frequently turned down. Photographers cleared for a ticket and a photo pass collect the ticket at Will Call, then meet an escort. They cannot take their seats until they finish shooting. Depending on the tour, gear may have to go back to the car or into a designated area between sets. The venue PR inbox is pr@asmtechport.com. (form undated) — [Boeing Center at Tech Port Media Request Form](https://form.jotform.com/221037353545047)
- **Aztec Theatre** (San Antonio): Live Nation operates the venue — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Theatre_(San_Antonio)). An SA Current item says House of Blues, a Live Nation unit, books its touring acts (undated) — [SA Current](https://www.sacurrent.com/?p=290880). No public media-request page was found.
- **Stable Hall** (307 Pearl Parkway, San Antonio): no public media-request process was found. The San Antonio Report describes it as a live-music room for hometown and national acts in a reworked, century-old building — [San Antonio Report](https://sanantonioreport.org/?p=5354955); [AXS venue listing](https://www.axs.com/venues/130021/stable-hall-san-antonio-tickets)
- **Moody Center** (Austin): UT owns it. Oak View Group operates it and keeps most income from non-UT events such as concerts for at least 35 years. UT has exclusive control 60 days a year — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody_Center). No concert media policy was found. UT Athletics' own policy (Feb 2025) covers athletics only: requests must come from a sports editor or director at an accredited outlet, and requests made within two days may not be considered — [UT Athletics](https://texaslonghorns.com/sports/2025/2/13/media-information-policies)
- **ACL Live at the Moody Theater** (the venue, not the festival): its press page asks for requests at least a week ahead and does not accept freelance photographer requests. FLAG: the page appears to be about 5 years old. — [ACL Live Press](https://www.acllive.com/press)
- **Stadium example (North Texas)**: REV Entertainment's credential terms call the credential a license that can be revoked at any time without cause. Holders get a narrow, non-transferable license limited to news and editorial use, and they agree not to sue and to indemnify REV. The page links to credential applications for Live Nation concerts at Globe Life Field (undated). — [REV Entertainment Credentials](https://reventertainment.com/credentials/)

**Promoter-level friction (older but on point)**
- Credentials arranged through a band member can end up at the box office with the photographer missing from the media list. The writer calls this a problem specific to Live Nation venues (Sept 23, 2019, FLAG older) — [PetaPixel open letter](https://petapixel.com/2019/09/23/dear-live-nation-an-open-letter-on-behalf-of-photographers-and-media/). The same author says Live Nation began requiring a waiver covering gear left unattended between sets — [Lizzy Davis](https://lizzydavis.com/photography/music-photography/music-industry/dear-live-nation-on-behalf-of-photographers-media/)

**What a request needs**
- Say who you are and whether you are asking for yourself or for an outlet. Include a website or Instagram link and a short background, where the photos will run, and the exact artist, date and venue. Be specific about what the coverage is worth to the act. (undated) — [Sheronovich: what to include](https://www.stephaniesheronovich.com/post/what-to-include-in-your-photo-pass-email-template-tips); [iShootShows templates](https://ishootshows.com/how-to-request-a-photo-pass-for-concert-photography-example-templates/)
- Faking an assignment is a serious mistake. Gatekeepers cut off anyone caught doing it (2025) — [DIY Photography](https://www.diyphotography.net/how-to-get-a-concert-photo-pass-breaking-into-live-music-photography/). Industry contacts know which outlets are real. Promising to sell shots to news agencies will not help unless you actually work for one. — [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/the-ultimate-guide-on-how-to-get-a-photo-pass/)
- Festival-grade criteria, from SXSW's 2027 form: an assignment from a legitimate outlet, plus the outlet's editorial focus, publishing record, audience engagement and the applicant's recent body of work (see Section 6). — [SXSW 2027 Press Application](https://form.jotform.com/sxsw/sxsw-2027-press-application)

**Lead times**
- Send the request 1 to 2 weeks before the show (2025) — [DIY Photography](https://www.diyphotography.net/how-to-get-a-concert-photo-pass-breaking-into-live-music-photography/). Arena approvals often come only in the final week or on show day — [Boeing Center form](https://form.jotform.com/221037353545047). ACL Live asked for at least a week (FLAG, old page) — [ACL Live](https://www.acllive.com/press)

**Why requests get denied**
- Pass allotments and pit spots are limited, so reps keep them for the outlets likely to deliver the strongest coverage. Some stages have no barricaded pit at all. — [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-photo-passes-for-concert-photography/)
- Outlet too small: a forum user was denied because the Danish website they shot for was considered too small (FLAG: Flickr thread, ~2008) — [Flickr Concert Photography group](https://www.flickr.com/groups/54089018@N00/discuss/72157608653880334/)
- An Instagram-only account with no written review behind it gives a publicist little reason to approve. Forum replies recommend building a site with written reviews of local acts before pitching PR. (Excerpt drew on the iShootShows and Flickr pages; exact attribution unverifiable.) — [Flickr thread](https://www.flickr.com/groups/54089018@N00/discuss/72157600193757220/72157612450725385); [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-photo-passes-for-concert-photography/)
- Day-of failures: an approved pass can fail at the door if nobody on site knows about it, so confirm a day-of contact. A venue may require a ticket on top of the pass. — same sources as above
- **2026 trend:** some major tours are opting out of outside photo coverage and relying on their own crews or the venue's in-house shooter. At TD Garden this year, Lady Gaga and Cardi B skipped outside photo coverage. The excerpt says the Globe's photo access "this year" amounted to only two passes; the scope of that figure is unclear. (May 7, 2026) — [Boston Globe, "The death of the photo pass"](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/05/07/arts/death-of-the-photo-pass/)

### Inferences
- The shooter's events fall into three tiers:
  - DIY and co-op house shows and release parties: approval comes from the promoter or artist directly.
  - Clubs and theaters (Stable Hall, Aztec): the promoter is the conduit, and the tour publicist decides at national-act shows. The Stable Hall pass shows that promoter-issued passes do happen.
  - Arenas and stadiums (Boeing Center at Tech Port, Alamodome, Frost Bank Center, Moody Center): the request goes through the venue PR form, the tour publicist decides, and the answer comes late. The Boeing Center form says plainly that independents without a publication are often denied.
- A strong request bundles:
  - the outlet name and URL;
  - an editor's assignment, or for the shooter's own outlet a masthead and a publish commitment;
  - a coverage plan (written review plus gallery within 24 to 48 hours, plus a short video recap if approved);
  - 2 or 3 links to comparable recent galleries;
  - audience numbers;
  - prior credits (SA Current internship 2024, Alamodome 2026, Stable Hall promoter pass);
  - a day-of phone or contact name.
- Send the request 1 to 2 weeks out, follow up in show week, and expect last-minute answers at arenas.
- The 2026 shift toward tour-controlled photography means fewer outside pit slots. The share of a shooter's work that comes through direct relationships with promoters, venues and artist teams will likely grow relative to cold publicist requests.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for how Live Nation, AEG Presents or C3 Presents process media requests (forms, regional PR roles, or whether local marketing teams can issue passes). Searches planned for this were cut off by the search budget.
- No public media policy was found for the Alamodome, Frost Bank Center, Stable Hall, Paper Tiger, Moody Center concerts, Stubb's or Emo's.
- Timing advice conflicts across guides: "1 to 2 weeks ahead" versus the observation that answers arrive in the final week. The excerpts did not resolve this.

## 2. Standard restrictions (first three songs, flash, pit vs. soundboard, escorts, headliner vs. openers) and how they differ by venue type

### Takeaway
The default is the first three songs with no flash, often escorted, and approval for the headliner does not automatically cover the openers. Arenas add escorts, gear stowage and seat rules. Festivals limit the pit to photo-credential holders and set their own flash and gear rules. A pass covers still photography, so video needs its own approval.

### Cited Findings
- The three-song window is the common default, and flash is typically off-limits. — [Adobe concert photography guide](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/type/concert-photography.html)
- Touring acts commonly ban professional cameras without explicit clearance. Backstage and tour-bus areas stay closed to photo-pass holders. A headliner's pass may not cover the openers, because the venue and the openers' managers decide that. (FLAG: one guide dated March 2009) — [iShootShows "The Photo Pass"](https://ishootshows.com/the-photo-pass/)
- Festival credential charts typically limit the photo pit to photographer credentials. Video crews may need positions next to the stage or a camera platform, so one generic media badge should not be issued to everyone with a camera. — [Wristband.com credential guide](https://www.wristband.com/blogs/news/media-press-and-photographer-event-credentials)
- **Arena procedure (San Antonio):** escort, ticket pickup at Will Call, no taking your seat until the shooting window ends, and gear sent to the car or a holding area between sets depending on the tour. — [Boeing Center at Tech Port Media Request Form](https://form.jotform.com/221037353545047)
- **SXSW showcases:**
  - Flash is allowed only during the first three songs of an artist's Music Festival set and banned after that.
  - Flash limits can vary by act or room, so posted signs and staff directions take priority.
  - No tripods, extra lights or bulky rigs in venue hallways.
  - SXSW's help center says professional gear is not allowed at Music Festival showcases, including the Outdoor Stage, without prior PR consent. It is unclear whether this applies to press or only to badge-holding attendees.
  - Sources: [SXSW Photo & Video Policies](https://sxsw.com/press-center/photo-video-policies/); [SXSW Help Center](https://support.sxsw.com/hc/en-us/articles/360020388631-I-want-to-take-photographs-or-record-video-at-SXSW-what-do-I-need-to-know)
- Some stages have no barricaded pit, so an approved pass can mean shooting from farther back. — [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-photo-passes-for-concert-photography/)
- Whether permission is needed at all depends on the act's level and the venue's size. — [Sheronovich](https://www.stephaniesheronovich.com/post/how-to-get-a-photo-pass-without-a-publication-yes-it-s-possible); [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/the-ultimate-guide-on-how-to-get-a-photo-pass/)
- Rights-grab contracts sometimes add restrictions. Ariana Grande's 2019 pit contract limited shooters to the first three songs and required her written sign-off for editorial use (FLAG 2019). — [Techdirt, Apr 2, 2019](https://www.techdirt.com/2019/04/02/ariana-grande-demands-all-photographers-her-concerts-transfer-copyright-to-her-nppa-revolts/)
- Photo ID is usually required at pickup, and a credential alone often does not get you in without a ticket. (General guides via search excerpt.) — [iShootShows arrival guide](https://ishootshows.com/concert-photography-101-arriving-picking-up-your-pass/)

### Inferences
- By venue type:
  - Clubs and DIY rooms usually have no pit and open floors. Flash norms there vary, and on-camera flash is often tolerated at DIY shows, but it should be cleared in writing whenever a pass is involved.
  - Theaters often have a pit or barricade, with first-three-songs rules.
  - Arenas and stadiums add escorts, seating and gear rules. "Soundboard-only" positions fit this tier, though it is not documented in the sources found (see Gaps).
  - Festivals add credential tiers and published rules (SXSW).
- An FX6 rig on a gimbal, monopod or tripod is likely to count as a "professional rig." SXSW bans tripods, monopods and extra lights for artist-side shooters (Section 7). Expect to need explicit written clearance for cinema-camera use, separate from the photo pass.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 source was found documenting "soundboard-only" shooting positions or how often arenas use them. It is a known practice but unsourced here.
- No source compared restrictions at Texas clubs, theaters and arenas specifically. Club-level norms in San Antonio and Austin (Paper Tiger, Mohawk, Empire Garage, etc.) were not found.

## 3. Using the shooter's own website, zine or Instagram as the requesting outlet

### Takeaway
A self-published outlet can work at club and theater level and with promoter-issued passes. It is the weakest position at arenas and major festivals. SXSW's 2027 form says personal-website photography does not count as an assignment, and it sends Instagram, TikTok or YouTube-first applicants to a separate Content Creator track. Credibility comes from written reviews plus galleries, a regular publishing cadence, audience reach and a record of coverage that actually got published.

### Cited Findings
- **SXSW 2027:**
  - Photography on spec, for a portfolio or for a personal site does not meet the assignment requirement.
  - One-person outlets still need a letter that covers the owner, and the 2027 form says personal-website photography is not a valid assignment. Solo operators should confirm with SXSW before relying on a self-issued letter.
  - Niche or trade outlets can be approved when their audience is clearly relevant and their coverage plan is substantive.
  - Applicants whose main platform is Instagram, TikTok, YouTube or LinkedIn go to a separate Content Creator application.
  - Sources: [SXSW 2027 Press Affiliation/Outlet Application](https://form.jotform.com/sxsw/sxsw-2027-press-application); [SXSW Press and Creator Accreditation](https://sxsw.com/press-accreditation/)
- **Arena:** independent photographers without an affiliated publication are frequently turned down, one request per outlet per event, and critic seats go to outlets writing a review. — [Boeing Center at Tech Port form](https://form.jotform.com/221037353545047)
- **It can work, but not easily:** one photographer got credentials for a Noah Kahan concert with no outlet behind them, and answers "not exactly" when asked whether it was easy (undated) — [Sheronovich](https://www.stephaniesheronovich.com/post/how-to-get-a-photo-pass-without-a-publication-yes-it-s-possible). A more skeptical guide says credentials go to people producing editorial coverage. PR staff occasionally credential individuals, but that guide sees little point in it. — [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/the-ultimate-guide-on-how-to-get-a-photo-pass/)
- **Be candid:** a reader comment recommends presenting yourself honestly as a freelancer who wants access, without implying a media affiliation you do not have. — [Sheronovich](https://www.stephaniesheronovich.com/post/how-to-get-a-photo-pass-without-a-publication-yes-it-s-possible)
- **Instagram alone is weak:** an Instagram-only account with no articles or written reviews gives a publicist little reason to approve. Advice is to build a site with written reviews of local acts first. — [Flickr thread](https://www.flickr.com/groups/54089018@N00/discuss/72157600193757220/72157612450725385); [iShootShows](https://ishootshows.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-photo-passes-for-concert-photography/)
- **Reach matters:** credentials are effectively traded for coverage, so an outlet's reach weighs heavily (example: the "too small" Danish website above). — [Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/groups/54089018@N00/discuss/72157608653880334/)
- **Creator tracks exist at Texas events:**
  - ACL Fest routes influencer and content-creator requests through a separate form — [ACL Fest support](https://support.aclfestival.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405461320084)
  - RODEOHOUSTON runs a content-creator credential: applications open Nov 15, 2026, are due Jan 15, 2027, and decisions go out Feb 12, 2027. The pass covers content creation only; backstage and meet-and-greets need explicit approval, and drones are banned. — [RODEOHOUSTON Content Creators](https://www.rodeohouston.com/content-creators/)

### Inferences
- **Credibility markers** (synthesized from the criteria above):
  - a review-plus-gallery format, not image dumps;
  - a steady cadence (for example weekly), with turnaround matching the shooter's 24 to 48 hours;
  - visible bylines and a masthead or "about" page with an editorial contact;
  - audience numbers (site sessions, newsletter subscribers, social reach) given in the request;
  - links to coverage of the same tier of act;
  - consistent tagging of artists, venues and promoters so publicists can see the published result.
- **Pros of self-publishing:** the shooter keeps the rights, sets turnaround, owns the audience relationship, and can cover scenes legacy outlets ignore (rage and underground rap, goth and rave, Latin DIY).
- **Cons:** weak at arena and festival level; at SXSW a personal-site letter is likely invalid; and the outlet does not pay.
- **Hybrid strategy:** keep the self-run outlet for club, DIY and promoter-pass shows, and get assignment letters from an established outlet (SA Current, Austin Chronicle, a national niche site) for SXSW, ACL and arena tours.

### Gaps
- No Reddit r/concertphotography threads (2024–2026) surfaced in search, so practitioner sentiment from Reddit is missing.
- No source gives numeric traffic or follower thresholds that publicists use.

## 4. Outlets that take freelance or contributor concert shooters (Texas and national niche) and what they pay

### Takeaway
In Texas, the SA Current and Austin Chronicle music sections run freelance photo credits, and Live From the Southside (a KSAT partner) recruits concert shooters. Texas Monthly keeps a 500-plus freelance pool but works by assignment. No Texas outlet's pay rate was found. Nationally, most contributor-driven music sites pay nothing and offer access and credit instead. Paid editorial work (older benchmarks: $150–$300 per concert day, $100–$200 per license) comes from established outlets by assignment.

## 7. Video at concerts: do video credentials exist, legitimate routes for videographers, and what is allowed

### Takeaway
Video credentials for independent outlets are rare and tightly capped. ## 8. Photo release forms and "rights grabs": what releases say, what NPPA/ASMP and working pros reject, and how pros respond

### Takeaway
There is no "standard" release. A 2021 UGA study of 284 concert photo agreements found that some terms limit editorial freedom and photographers' copyright. Clauses pros reject include copyright transfer or work-for-hire, moral-rights waivers, free perpetual promotional use, pre-publication approval, usage time limits, full-file delivery and gear confiscation. Responses that work include declining, as individual shooters and whole agencies have done (Marcus King 2024, Oasis 2025), and negotiating, as the NPPA did with Taylor Swift in 2015 and with a sports firm in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**Recent cases (2024–2026)**
- **Nov 2024, Marcus King:** a photographer walked away from a Marcus King show in London rather than sign paperwork demanding copyright waivers and high-resolution copies within a week. Moral-rights waivers were also demanded, which PetaPixel reads as meaning no credit and no payment. The same writer flagged similar terms from Foo Fighters, Elle King and Night Ranger. — [PetaPixel, Nov 7, 2024](https://petapixel.com/2024/11/07/music-photographers-up-in-arms-over-rights-grab-contracts); [AI-AP](https://www.ai-ap.com/publications/article/34174/business-report-music-photographers-up-in-arms-ov.html); [Metaltalk](https://www.metaltalk.net/marcus-king-enforces-strict-copyright-contract-claiming-full-ownership-of-concert-photos.php)
- **July 2025, Oasis Live '25:**
  - Outlets were limited to 12 months' use of the opening-night photos, after which rights reverted to the band. The first proposal was one month.
  - The NMC filed a formal complaint (July 9, 2025). Its chief executive argued that news relevance "doesn't expire after 365 days."
  - Reuters, Getty, AP and others skipped Heaton Park, and NMC members later said they would skip the rest of the tour.
  - Sources: [PetaPixel, Jul 7, 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/07/07/british-rock-band-oasis-demand-photo-agencies-lose-their-image-rights-after-one-year/); [NMC](https://www.newsmediacoalition.org/test-2/); [NMC](https://www.newsmediacoalition.org/major-news-organisations-suspend-news-photo-coverage-of-oasis-concerts-after-band-imposes-restrictions-on-editors/); [NMC on impact](https://www.newsmediacoalition.org/oasis-tour-photo-restrictions-impact-scope-and-visibility-of-news-coverage/)
- **Oct 2025, NPPA versus Gazelle Group** (sports credentials, but the template is relevant):
  - The original terms would have given Gazelle a free, irrevocable right to use any event photo, excluding resale.
  - NPPA's counter-draft (Oct 14) kept ownership with the photographers and let Gazelle seek, but not require, a separate non-exclusive license for narrow uses.
  - Gazelle's revision still claimed up to three chosen photos under a non-exclusive license, and NPPA counsel still objected on Oct 23.
  - Source: [PetaPixel, Oct 28, 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/10/28/nppa-warns-photographers-against-rights-grabs-in-credential-agreements/)
- **May 2026 commentary:** a contract is typically the next hurdle once a pass is granted. Terms now include full ownership, pre-publication approval, editing bans and after-the-fact image picks (opinion blog). — [Slurp-Ramen, May 10, 2026](https://www.slurp-ramen.com/blog/concert-photography-is-dying-and-nobody-seems-to-care/)

**Research and background**
- UGA Grady College (Kyser Lough; 2021 peer-reviewed conference paper) analyzed 284 concert photography agreements and describes itself as the first study of their details. It found that some terms "begin to limit editorial freedoms and copyright ownership," a "problematic form of image control." Clause frequencies were not retrieved. — [Grady College](https://grady.uga.edu/research/sign-this-or-go-home-concert-photography-agreements-as-restrictive-image-control-devices/); [Lough CV](https://www.kyserlough.com/cv.html)
- Texas-based Texas Music Photographers argues that calling these agreements "standard" is misleading because no industry-wide version exists. It focuses on copyright and indemnity clauses (about 5 years old). — [Texas Music Photographers](https://www.texasmusicphotographers.com/post/what-is-a-standard-photography-agreement)
- iShootShows warns against contracts that transfer all rights, including copyright, in perpetuity, and flags moral-rights (droit moral) waivers as a frequent problem (not legal advice). — [iShootShows releases guide](https://ishootshows.com/concert-photography-rights-contracts-photo-releases/)
- Agreements govern use and whether the artist must approve images before publication (Canadian law firm). — [Cassels](https://cassels.com/insights/live-forever-ownership-and-usage-of-concert-photos/)
- **Texas credential terms:** a revocable license, editorial-only use, a promise not to sue, and indemnity. — [REV Entertainment](https://reventertainment.com/credentials/)

**Earlier benchmark cases (FLAG older)**
- **2019, Ariana Grande:** every frame was classed as work made for hire with copyright going to her company, shooters were limited to three songs, and editorial use needed her written approval. NPPA and 15 other groups objected, and NPPA counsel called it an overreach. — [NPPA](https://nppa.org/news/nppa-joined-15-other-groups-protests-ariana-grande-photo-agreement); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ariana-grande-louis-c-k-concert-rules-push-copyright-limits)
- **2015, Taylor Swift:** five Montreal newsrooms boycotted over worldwide, perpetual free use and gear seizure or destruction terms. After NPPA counsel's involvement, the gear-destruction language became a request to delete photos. — [The Story Board](https://www2.thestoryboard.ca/storyboard/page/article-content/newspapers-reject-overreaching-freelance-photography-contracts); [PetaPixel, Jul 22, 2015](https://petapixel.com/2015/07/22/taylor-swifts-concert-photo-contract-changed-to-be-more-photographer-friendly/)
- **2011, Lady Gaga:** the release assigned copyright to her, and the photographer's editor told him to skip both the form and the show. The form also barred print and web use beyond four months. — [PetaPixel, Mar 7, 2011](https://petapixel.com/2011/03/07/brouhaha-over-lady-gaga-rights-grab/)

### Inferences
- **Red-flag checklist for the shooter:**
  - copyright assignment or work-for-hire;
  - moral-rights waivers (no credit);
  - free, perpetual or "any media" promotional use by the artist, promoter or venue;
  - pre-publication approval or editing restrictions;
  - usage time limits;
  - delivery of all files or high-res within X days;
  - a ban on licensing to third parties;
  - gear confiscation or destruction;
  - broad indemnity.
- **Response ladder:** read the release before show day (ask the publicist to send it in advance) → strike or amend clauses → offer a limited non-exclusive license (for example a few selected images for the artist's social media, with credit, no commercial or merch use) → decline and shoot from the crowd only where camera policy allows.
- For video, also watch for "footage handover" clauses like the North Texas Fair & Rodeo's (Section 6).
- No ASMP statement specific to concerts surfaced. NPPA's 2025 counter-draft (photographer keeps ownership; any license is separate, optional and narrow) is the clearest current model language.

## 9. How concert photography turns into paid work (licensing pass images, publicist relationships), with examples

### Takeaway
A photo pass grants access, not a license. Money comes from a separate agreement with the outlet (per image or flat fee), or from licensing images to the artist, label or promoter, priced by usage. Releases that grab rights can wipe out that second income stream. Recent cases show the need for written terms before merch or promotional use. As tours keep more photography in-house (2026), relationships with publicists and promoters built on reliable, fast, credited delivery become the path to being hired.

### Cited Findings
- The outlet that got you credentialed is the fastest source of cash, usually paying per picture or a flat assignment fee (Nov 2025) — [CLIMB](https://climbtheladder.com/how-to-become-a-concert-photographer-and-get-photo-passes/)
- Labels and promoters may pay to license images for album covers, social posts, posters and merch. Settle usage terms before the shoot. — [PhotoWorkout](https://www.photoworkout.com/how-to-become-a-band-photographer/)
- Pricing scales with the breadth and length of the band's use (about 2022) — [David Simchock](https://www.davidsimchock.com/blog/music-photography-why-a-photographer-may-get-paid-more-than-a-band-for-shooting-a-gig). Older anchors: $100 minimum for posters or shirts, more for big tour runs; $100–$200 per photo license to a publication (FLAG 2017) — [PetaPixel 2017](https://petapixel.com/2017/02/08/concert-photography-jobs-make-money-shooting-music/)
- Posters and merch need explicit permission and possibly a model release (Sept 2025) — [DIY Photography copyright guide](https://www.diyphotography.net/concert-photography-copyright-what-every-photographer-should-know/). UK guidance warns that assignment clauses can remove your ability to license your own shots. — [Blanc Creative](https://blanc-creative.com/music/who-owns-your-concert-photos/)
- **Cautionary case (Dec 2025):** a photographer alleges a metal band put her Copenhell festival photo on a collectible coin without permission. She says the shot was an editorial commission, she never signed a license, she could not reach the band's reps, and she turned down a settlement she considered unfair. No band response was reported. — [PetaPixel, Dec 22, 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/12/22/photographer-accuses-metal-band-of-using-her-image-on-merch-without-permission/)
- The rare extra access usually goes to photographers the performer hires to cover the whole show. — [Wikipedia: Concert photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_photography)
- **Career examples:**
  - A San Antonio shooter worked 5+ years as The Freeman Coliseum's house photographer before freelancing and joining Live From the Southside (2024) — [KSAT](https://ksat.com/news/local/2024/09/06/southside-spotlight-chelsea-braun)
  - Music photographer Adam Parshall branched into business consulting for local bands, plus tour and merch management (undated interview) — [Photographer Tonight](https://www.photographertonight.com/post/adam-parshall-interview)
  - A guide updated in 2025 says the real money comes from publications or tour work, and copyright largely blocks mass-selling of concert shots. — [Greeblehaus](https://greeblehaus.com/become-a-concert-photographer/)
- Freelancing is the norm, with income from licensing or individual gigs; a smaller group holds salaried outlet jobs — [Careers in Music](https://www.careersinmusic.com/music-photographer). Paid concert gigs are scarce and the music stock market is crowded (FLAG 2011) — [A Photo Editor](https://www.aphotoeditor.com/2011/02/03/making-money-after-shooting-concerts-for-free/)
- Many photographers start with unpaid work for small outlets to gain access and portfolio material. — [Photographer Tonight](https://www.photographertonight.com/post/how-to-turn-music-photography-into-your-full-time-business); [Format](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/concert-photography-live-music-tips)
- **2026 context:** tours lean on their own crews and in-house shooters. — [Boston Globe, May 7, 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/05/07/arts/death-of-the-photo-pass/)

### Inferences
- **Practical loop for this shooter:**
  1. Deliver the outlet piece within 24–48h and tag the artist, publicist, promoter and venue.
  2. Send the publicist or promoter a short selects link with a clear licensing offer: an editorial and social-media use license on stated terms; merch, ads and album use quoted separately.
  3. Keep a written usage log.
  4. Never sign a release that pre-grants promotional rights for free, since that is the product being sold.
- Video recaps (30–45 seconds, cleared) offer artists and promoters something stills shooters cannot. That makes them a natural upsell into paid content work, which other researchers cover.
